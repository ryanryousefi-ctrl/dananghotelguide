Getting Around Da Nang

Grab covers most trips. Here's when to use everything else.

Updated 2026 · Local editor · Real 2026 prices

The Single Most Important App You Will Download in Vietnam

Download Grab. Use Grab. Do nothing else.

This is not a suggestion. This is the difference between a great trip and one where you get quoted four times the fair price by a taxi driver the moment you step outside arrivals.

Avoid all taxis. Avoid anyone who walks up to you outside your hotel and says they will match Grab. Avoid the tuk-tuk, the waved-down motorbike, and the guy at the airport who "has a friend with a car." None of them will match Grab. They are counting on you not knowing what the real price is.

Grab is a car-sharing app - think Uber, but faster, cheaper, and more reliable than Uber ever was anywhere. You open the app, you enter your destination, you see the price before you confirm. That price is what you pay. No negotiation. No surprise charges. No language barrier. The car arrives in less time than it takes to put on your shoes.

Here is what the numbers look like in Da Nang. Nothing in the city should cost you more than 50,000 VND - that is $2. Airport to your hotel should run no more than 200,000 VND. A trip to Hoi An is around 250,000 to 300,000 VND. That is the ceiling for virtually every journey you will make. At those prices there is no reason to shop around, cut corners, or try to bargain with anyone.

Grab also doubles as the fastest food delivery app in existence. Order from a local restaurant, your food arrives in 15 minutes, and the cost including delivery is less than a coffee back home.

No tip is required, but you can add one in the app after drop-off if your driver was great. Most of them are.

Do not get cute about this. Download Grab before you land. It is the best travel advice on this entire website.

Every Way to Get Around

When Grab Is Not Enough

For most visitors on a short trip, Grab handles everything. But not everyone is on a short trip, and not every situation fits a ride-share.

Renting a motorbike is the classic Da Nang move - cheap, fast, and the best way to explore Son Tra Peninsula on your own schedule. The catch is your license. Technically you need an International Driving Permit endorsed for motorbikes. In practice enforcement is inconsistent, but an accident without the right license means your travel insurance will not pay out. Know that going in.

A private car with a driver is worth considering if you are planning a full day out - Marble Mountains, Hoi An, Hai Van Pass, all in one loop. Calling four separate Grabs across a day works fine, but a private car means one driver, one price agreed upfront, and no waiting around between stops. It is a genuine convenience, not a scam, if you book through your hotel or a reputable local operator.

Walking is underrated. The beach strip between An Thuong and the shore is completely walkable. The Han River promenade is flat and pleasant at night. Da Nang is not a city that punishes you for going on foot - at least in the areas most visitors actually spend time in.

But for everything else - airport runs, evening meals, day trips, getting back after a late night - Grab is the answer. Always.

Grab

Best Option

Vietnam's dominant ride-hail app. Available city-wide, 2–5 minute wait times. GrabCar (air-conditioned) for comfort; GrabBike for short hops. No language required — destination is typed in app.

City rides: 40,000–80,000 VND · To Hoi An: ~250,000 VND · Airport: 60,000–120,000 VND

Taxi

Fine, Use Meter

Vinasun and Mai Linh are the reliable metered companies. Always insist on the meter — accept no fixed-price offers from strangers at the airport or near tourist areas. Grab is usually cheaper and easier.

Similar to Grab. Flag-fall ~15,000 VND, then ~12,000–15,000 VND/km
Skip unlicensed taxis approaching you at the airport — fixed rates are rarely in your favour.

Scooter Rental

If Experienced

100,000–200,000 VND per day from rental shops near My Khe. Freedom to explore Son Tra and Hai Van Pass at your own pace. Requires comfort with Vietnamese traffic — faster and less orderly than most Western cities.

100,000–200,000 VND/day · Fuel: ~25,000 VND/litre
Skip if you're uncertain — Grab is cheap enough that rental rarely saves meaningful money for a short trip.

Private Car

Best for Day Trips

Book a full-day private car with driver for Hoi An, Hai Van Pass, or Ba Na Hills. Hotel concierge can arrange; rates are negotiable. More comfortable than Grab for long distances, and the driver waits while you explore.

Hoi An full day: ~1,200,000–1,800,000 VND · Ba Na Hills: ~1,000,000–1,500,000 VND

Walking

For Compact Areas

An Thuong neighbourhood and the My Khe beachfront are walkable. The city center (Han River area) is walkable once you're there. Between major zones — beach to city, city to Son Tra — the distances require a ride.

Free · Best before 10am and after 5pm (heat)

Airport Transfer

Use Grab

Da Nang airport is 3–5km from the city center, 5–8km from My Khe. Open the Grab app on arrival — it works in the terminal. Faster and cheaper than hotel transfers for most destinations.

To My Khe: 60,000–120,000 VND · 10–20 minutes
Hotel airport transfers average 200,000–400,000 VND — rarely worth it.

Common Questions

What is the best way to get around Da Nang?
Grab for virtually everything. Reliable, cheap, no language needed. Most city rides cost 40,000–80,000 VND.
How much does a Grab cost in Da Nang?
Short city rides: 30,000–60,000 VND. My Khe to city center: 50,000–80,000 VND. Da Nang to Hoi An: 200,000–280,000 VND. Da Nang to Marble Mountains: 70,000–110,000 VND. Prices vary with demand.
Should I rent a scooter in Da Nang?
Only if you're already comfortable riding in Vietnamese traffic. Rentals cost 100,000–200,000 VND per day. If uncertain, use Grab — it's cheap enough that a scooter rarely saves meaningful money on a short trip.
How far is Da Nang airport from the city?
3–5km from the city center, 5–8km from My Khe Beach hotels. Grab takes 10–20 minutes and costs 60,000–120,000 VND. One of the most convenient airports in Vietnam.
