The honest case

Why stay overnight?

Hoi An is much more atmospheric before day-trippers arrive and after they leave. The Ancient Town before 8am is genuinely quiet. Between 11am and 3pm in peak season, the lanes fill up fast.

Staying overnight gives you access to early mornings in the old quarter, lantern evenings on the river, and An Bang Beach without the midday crowd. Two nights is the minimum to feel like you've actually been there.

If you're day-tripping from Da Nang, take the first Grab and eat breakfast there. You'll be glad you did.

Hoi An Ancient Town

"The Ancient Town before 8am is genuinely magical. After 11am in peak season, it's a different place entirely."

UNESCO World Heritage Site

The Ancient Town

The pedestrianised historic core: tailors, lantern shops, cao lau restaurants, Assembly Halls, the Japanese Covered Bridge. Tourists concentrate here, especially from midday. No motor vehicles during certain hours. An Old Town entrance ticket (120,000 VND, includes 5 attraction visits) covers most of the major sites — walking the lanes themselves is free. Buy at the entrance points near the bridge.

What to eat

Hoi An food

The food scene here punches well above the town's size. Four things to eat before you leave:

The essentials

Cao lầu — thick noodles in a rich broth with pork and crispy rice crackers, unique to Hoi An. The water supposedly comes from a specific well.

White rose dumplings (bánh bao vạc) — translucent, delicate, almost too pretty to eat. Worth tracking down.

Bánh mì — Hoi An's version is widely considered the best in Vietnam. Phương on Phan Châu Trinh is the famous one.

Cơm gà — shredded chicken rice cooked in the broth. Simple, excellent, very local.

Planning your trip

Day trip or overnight?

Both work. Here's when to choose each.

Day trip from Da Nang

Good for Ancient Town highlights, a bowl of cao lau, and a few hours of sightseeing. Take the first Grab of the day — you'll arrive before the tour buses. Leave by 3pm or stay for the evening lanterns. Costs around 200,000–300,000 VND each way by Grab.

Stay overnight

Better for lantern evenings, early morning walks through empty lanes, An Bang Beach, a cooking class, and the actual atmosphere that makes Hoi An special. Two nights is the minimum. Three is more comfortable. You'll spend more on the hotel but save on repeated Grab fares and actually see the place properly.

Getting there

From Da Nang to Hoi An

Roughly 30–45 minutes by road, depending on traffic and your starting point in Da Nang. The distance is around 25–30km. There is no direct train into Hoi An — the nearest rail station is in Da Nang city. Grab car is the easiest option for most travellers (200,000–300,000 VND). Private transfer from the airport to Hoi An typically costs $15–25 USD. Shuttle buses exist but add 60–90 minutes and involve stops.

Common questions

Hoi An FAQ

Is Hoi An worth visiting in 2026?
Absolutely. Hoi An remains one of the most atmospheric towns in Southeast Asia. The Ancient Town is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, the food scene is excellent, and boutique hotels here deliver genuine character at prices that are still reasonable by international standards. The town has become more tourist-heavy since 2019 but it still delivers. Visit in February to May for the best combination of weather and manageable crowds.
How far is Hoi An from Da Nang?
Hoi An is approximately 30km south of Da Nang city centre, and around 25km from Da Nang International Airport. By Grab car or private transfer, the journey usually takes 30–40 minutes depending on traffic. The road is good and well-signed, passing through Marble Mountains and Non Nuoc beach. See the Da Nang Airport to Hoi An guide for all transport options.
How many days do you need in Hoi An?
Two nights is the minimum to feel like you've actually been there. Three nights is comfortable — you can explore the Ancient Town properly, visit An Bang Beach, do a cooking class, and have time for a tailor. If you want to slow down, read by the pool, and eat well without a schedule, five nights goes quickly.
Is Hoi An expensive?
Hoi An sits in a middle range for Vietnam. Street food is still cheap (30,000–60,000 VND for cao lau). Tourist restaurants are 20–40% pricier than Da Nang equivalents. Hotels often deliver better value than Da Nang at the mid-range level — a boutique hotel with a pool on the river runs $60–90, and you're within walking distance of the Ancient Town. The main unexpected cost is tailoring, where it's easy to spend more than planned. See Is Hoi An cheaper than Da Nang? for the full breakdown.
Do I need a ticket to enter the Ancient Town?
Yes. Visitors need a Hoi An Old Town ticket (currently 120,000 VND) to enter most historical sites — Assembly Halls, the Japanese Covered Bridge, communal houses, and museums. The ticket includes 5 attraction visits. Walking the streets of the Ancient Town itself is free, but you'll need the ticket to go inside the key sites. Buy at the ticket offices near the entrance points.
