MICHELIN Star

La Maison 1888

Da Nang's only MICHELIN Star restaurant sits in a restored colonial villa at the InterContinental Sun Peninsula Resort, cascading down a forested hillside above a private bay. The cuisine is French fine dining conceived with Pierre Gagnaire's influence — precise technique, Vietnamese ingredients, a wine list with genuine depth.

The setting is among the most dramatic in Southeast Asia. Reserve the table by the window. Book dinner weeks in advance in peak season; the resort runs a complimentary city transfer when you call ahead.

Son Tra Peninsula · $$$$ · Tasting menu from $120

Nén Restaurant

Central Vietnamese cuisine built on a sustainable sourcing philosophy. The menu changes with the season and the available catch — the kitchen works directly with fishing families and small farms in the region. Bún bò Huế, mì Quảng, and bánh xèo reinterpreted with careful technique and far better ingredients than most versions of the same dishes.

The dining room is warm and unhurried. Prices are genuinely fair for the quality. The restaurant sits up a short staircase in a villa with the windows open to the night air. Book 48 hours ahead on weekends — it sells out.

Hai Chau · 2 Dong Da Street · $$ · Mains from $8

Da Nang's defining dish

Start with mì Quảng.

Turmeric-stained rice noodles, a shallow broth with pork and shrimp, crushed peanuts, a sesame rice cracker balanced on top, fresh herbs piled beside it. Da Nang's signature bowl costs $2.50 at a MICHELIN-recognised stall. Order it for breakfast at Mì Quảng 1A on Hai Phong Street before 9am, when the kitchen still has stock.

What Da Nang's MICHELIN Scene Actually Means

Da Nang got its first MICHELIN Guide in 2023. The reaction from long-term residents was mostly positive — the city's Central Vietnamese food tradition finally getting formal recognition — with some concern about what external validation does to local prices.

Two years on, the impact has been measured. Bib Gourmand prices are still low, the neighbourhood stalls haven't repositioned as tourist restaurants, and La Maison 1888 was always beyond the price range of the average local diner anyway. What the Guide did was give visitors a framework for navigating a food culture that was already excellent. The mì Quảng has always been this good. Now more people know to order it.

Where you stay changes where you'll eat.

Da Nang Dining — Common Questions

What food is Da Nang famous for? +

Mì Quảng (turmeric noodle with pork, shrimp, and herbs), bánh mì (Da Nang's version uses a wider bread with more generous filling than Saigon-style), bánh xèo (sizzling rice crepe with pork and bean sprouts), and fresh seafood grilled on the beachfront. The city also has a strong cafe culture and excellent bánh tráng cuốn thịt heo (rice paper rolls with pork belly).

Where is the best area to eat in Da Nang? +

An Thuong neighbourhood behind My Khe Beach has the highest concentration of quality restaurants per block — Korean BBQ, Vietnamese seafood, international options, and cafes all within walking distance. For local Vietnamese food at local prices, the streets around Han Market (Han Thuyen, Ong Ich Khiem) have excellent street-food options. The full MICHELIN cluster is spread across Hai Chau and Son Tra.

How much does food cost in Da Nang? +

Local Vietnamese: 40,000–80,000 VND ($1.60–3.20) for a full meal with drink. Tourist-area restaurants: 150,000–350,000 VND ($6–14) per person. Seafood restaurants: 300,000–800,000 VND ($12–32) per person. Hotel restaurants: 400,000–1,200,000 VND ($16–48) per person. The quality-to-price ratio at the local end is among the best in Southeast Asia. See the Da Nang budget guide for a full breakdown.
