Editor's Top Pick

Han Market

Hải Châu · Souvenirs · Tailoring · Local Food · Mixed Local/Tourist

This is the easiest market for a first-time visitor who wants souvenirs, tailoring, and a real local-market feel without traveling far from the city center. Han Market sits on the Han River at 119 Trần Phú Street: manageable in scale, an easy Grab ride, and open to tourists without requiring any Vietnamese.

The ground floor is the best part: fresh produce, local food stalls serving mì Quảng and bún bò at 7am, sugarcane juice. The upper floors are souvenirs and tailoring, with quality that varies stall to stall and prices that are always negotiable. Go before 10am, when it's cooler and the food stalls are still running. For serious tailoring, Hoi An's workshops go deeper, but Han Market works fine for straightforward pieces on a short timeline.

119 Trần Phú St, Hải Châu 6am – 7pm daily Best before 10am Ground floor: food · Upper: souvenirs + tailoring

Skip if: all you want is cheap local prices and you don't need tourist-facing service.

Shopping guide

What Is Actually Worth Buying

Vietnamese Coffee

Good quality, and it packs easily for the flight home. Robusta blends from the Central Highlands are the local standard. Con Market has the best prices; Han Market has the widest packaged range.

Buy at: Con Market or Han Market
Dried Fruit & Local Snacks

Mango, jackfruit, tamarind candy, and mixed dried fruit. Better quality and better prices at Con Market than at the tourist-facing shops.

Buy at: Con Market
Silk Lanterns

The signature Hoi An buy, handmade locally in dozens of sizes and colors. The selection at Hoi An Night Market beats anything you'll find in Da Nang. Stick to the smaller sizes if you need it to survive your suitcase.

Buy at: Hoi An Night Market
Spices

Star anise, cardamom, turmeric, cinnamon sticks: all lightweight and genuinely local. Sold by weight at Con Market and Hoi An Central Market.

Buy at: Con Market or Hoi An Central Market
Tailored Clothing

Worth doing in Hoi An if you have 48+ hours and a clear reference garment to hand over. Han Market is faster but shallower in quality. Agree on the final price before any work begins.

Buy at: Han Market (fast) or Hoi An tailor street (quality)
Lacquerware & Ceramics

Bowls, trays, chopsticks, and decorative pieces. Check the finish before you buy: mould-cast items can pass for hand-painted ones if you're not looking closely. Hoi An generally has better quality at similar prices.

Buy at: Han Market or Hoi An
Timing

When to Go

Morning

6am – 9am

Con Market · Han Market · Hoi An Central Market

This is the best window at all three local markets. Cooler temperatures, the freshest produce, the most local activity, and every food stall is running. Han Market's ground-floor breakfast scene peaks between 7 and 8:30am. Hoi An Central Market is largely wound down by 10am.

Evening & Night

7pm – 9:30pm

Son Tra Night Market · Hoi An Night Market

Peak atmosphere at both night markets. Son Tra pairs well with the Dragon Bridge fire show on Saturday and Sunday evenings. Hoi An Night Market is at its best between 7 and 8:30pm, before the crowds get thick. Both stay open until 11pm but thin out after 9:30.

How to Bargain Without Being Annoying

At souvenir and clothing stalls, bargaining is expected. Open at 50–60% of the asking price, stay friendly, and be willing to walk away. Sellers know their floor, and they'll tell you when you've hit it. Settling around 60–65% of the opening price is realistic for most items.

Don't bargain at food stalls with displayed prices, those are fixed. Don't bargain aggressively over amounts under 20,000 VND (under $1): the friction isn't worth it, and it reads poorly. At Con Market, produce prices are already close to genuine local rates and less padded than the tourist-facing stalls at Han. Simple rule: if you wouldn't negotiate at a supermarket, don't negotiate here.

Quick comparison

Da Nang or Hoi An for Markets?

Da Nang

Everyday & Local

Better for working markets, food at local prices, and the daily rhythm of how people here actually shop. Con Market in particular is one of central Vietnam's largest real markets. Han Market is the right first stop for visitors who want souvenirs and tailoring without making the trip to Hoi An.

Hoi An

Atmosphere & Crafts

Stronger for lanterns, tailoring quality, silk goods, and the evening market experience. The Night Market on Nguyễn Hoàng Street is the most atmospheric market in central Vietnam, and the Da Nang equivalent doesn't come close. The Central Market in the morning is worth combining with a cooking class or an early breakfast walk.

Where to stay

Staying Near the Markets?

Han Market and Con Market are easiest to reach from Han River hotels, both are walkable from the Bạch Đằng riverside strip. My Khe beach hotels are typically a 10-15 minute Grab away. For Hoi An, Old Town hotels put you within walking distance of the Central Market and the Night Market. See our full guide to where to stay in Da Nang or where to stay in Hoi An.

Common Questions

Which market is best in Da Nang?
Han Market is the best first market for visitors: central, tourist-friendly, and good for souvenirs, tailoring, and a local breakfast at 7am. Con Market is bigger, cheaper, and more authentic, but it takes some comfort navigating without English. Go to Han Market first. Go to Con Market for a more local experience or lower prices.
Is Han Market or Con Market better?
Depends what you're after. Han Market is easier for tourists: central location, English-speaking vendors, good souvenir range, and excellent ground-floor food stalls. Con Market is Da Nang's largest and most authentic, with prices 20–40% lower, but minimal English and a rawer atmosphere. For souvenirs: Han Market. For food and local experience: Con Market.
What should I buy at Da Nang markets?
Vietnamese coffee, dried fruit and snacks, spices, lacquerware, and silk scarves are the reliable picks. Tailored clothing is worth considering at Han Market if you have 24–48 hours. In Hoi An, silk lanterns are the signature buy: handmade locally and genuinely better there than anywhere else in Vietnam.
Do I need to bargain at Da Nang markets?
For souvenirs and clothing, yes, bargaining is expected. Start at 50–60% of the asking price and settle somewhere between. For food stalls with displayed prices, the price is fixed. At Con Market, produce prices are closer to genuine local rates. Don't bargain aggressively over small amounts. It's not worth the friction.
Which market is best for food?
Con Market has the best street food concentration: bún chả cá, mì Quảng, bánh tráng cuốn, and chè at local prices. Han Market's ground floor is also excellent for a local breakfast, with mì Quảng and sugarcane juice at 7am. For Hoi An food, the Central Market in the morning is the source for cao lầu, cơm gà, and the freshest ingredients.
What is the best night market in Da Nang?
Son Tra Night Market near the Dragon Bridge is Da Nang's main evening market: accessible, good grilled seafood, and it pairs naturally with a Dragon Bridge visit on Saturday and Sunday evenings. It lacks the atmosphere of Hoi An's Night Market but is a decent option. For a more atmospheric evening, the trip to Hoi An is worth it.
Are Da Nang markets cash only?
Yes. Markets in Da Nang and Hoi An run almost entirely on cash. Carry small denominations, 20,000 to 100,000 VND notes, since many vendors can't make change for large bills. ATMs are available near Han Market on Bạch Đằng Street. Bring enough cash before you go.
