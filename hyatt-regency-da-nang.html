Hyatt Regency Danang Resort and Spa

5-Star Beach Resort · Non Nuoc Beach

“An ocean view you can watch from the bathtub, and a beach that's actually quiet before 9am.”

9.0/ 10
Editorial Score

Quick Verdict

Book it if you want the most reliably good all-round beach resort in Da Nang, not the most dramatic (that's the InterContinental) and not the most intimate (that's Naman Retreat), but the one that delivers across every category that actually makes a resort holiday work. Five pools mean you can always find a quiet corner, Non Nuoc Beach is finer and calmer than the My Khe strip further north, and the breakfast buffet, especially the made-to-order pho, is worth setting an alarm for. The catch is distance: you're 12km from the city, so Dragon Bridge and Han Market street food both mean a Grab ride, and this only works if you're planning a beach-first trip rather than an evenings-in-the-city one.

Our Take

I wasn't planning to be up at 6:30, but the light coming through the curtains was doing something I hadn't expected, and I pulled them open to find the South China Sea sitting there in the early morning grey, completely flat, the beach below empty in both directions. What I hadn't realised until I arrived is how much of this resort you can see from a single ocean-view room. The balcony looks over the pool complex toward the sea, and the bathtub, deep and freestanding, positioned directly in front of a floor-to-ceiling window, looks at the same view. You can lie in it and watch the waves. That sounds like something a brochure would claim. It's just true.

The Hyatt Regency is the resort I'd send most people to if they asked me to recommend one luxury beachfront hotel in Da Nang with no caveats. Not the most dramatic property in the city, not the most intimate, but the most reliably good across every dimension that actually determines whether a resort holiday works. The room delivers what the photos promise. The beach, a quieter stretch of coast known as Non Nuoc, is genuinely fine sand, cleaner and softer than the sections further north. And the breakfast, particularly the made-to-order pho and build-your-own banh mi bar, turns into an hour every time without you planning it that way.

The Rooms

The Rooms

Ocean view rooms run around 52 square metres, big enough that you stop noticing the size and start noticing the details: a walk-in wardrobe that keeps clothes off the chairs, a balcony table and chairs that get actual use rather than sitting decorative, a rain shower that runs properly hot. The welcome tray's dark chocolate chip cookies are crumbly with a good chew and a hint of salt, a small thing, but it sets a tone.

One honest note: wifi drops on the balcony and doesn't reach the bathroom, worth knowing if you're working remotely. The TV also occasionally cuts out. Neither ruins the stay, but you should know going in. Which room would we choose? A Premier Ocean View on a higher floor. On clear mornings you can pick out the Lady Buddha statue on Son Tra Peninsula in the distance, a detail most guests never think to look for.

The Reason to Book It

Five Pools

The main lagoon pool snakes through the resort almost like a river, with sections you can swim between, but it's the smaller pool closer to the beach that pulls people back in the afternoons. By 4pm the families have mostly drifted in for naps and early dinners, and you can get a run of it nearly to yourself, the light dropping, the sea twenty metres away, the water finally feeling right after the midday heat.

There's also a sand-bottom pool, genuinely unusual for a resort this size, and a shallower pool near the beach that's better suited to children. During Vietnamese public holidays the main pool gets crowded. It's a large resort and the system handles it, but if you want the quiet version of the Hyatt, avoid Tet and the April holiday period.

Pool + Beach

Pool + Beach

The best moment of a stay here often comes on the second morning, before breakfast, walking the path through the grounds and reaching Non Nuoc Beach with barely anyone else there. The sand at this stretch of coast is genuinely fine, cleaner and softer than the sections further north near the Hilton and Novotel, and the beach service team sets up loungers and umbrellas before most guests are even awake. By mid-morning the beach fills in, not unpleasantly, there's space, but the early version is a different thing entirely. If you're a light sleeper and the resort wakes you early, walk to the beach instead of going back to sleep.

Food + Breakfast

Food + Drink

Breakfast is the kind of sprawling resort buffet where a quick coffee somehow turns into an hour. The Vietnamese stations are the reason: pho made to order, a banh mi bar where you build your own, and a sticky rice station worth returning to every morning. The pastry section has croissants that are actually airy and flaky rather than the dense approximations most resort buffets serve.

Book a table at the Pool House if you want the quieter version, it runs its own breakfast menu alongside the main buffet, Vietnamese or Western, and it's noticeably calmer than the main restaurant. The practical note: 8 to 9:30am is the rush, with wait times posted for tables. Go at 7 or after 10 if you want to take your time.

Who It's For

Families, Couples & Who It Suits

Most guests here, consistently around 70-75 percent at breakfast, are Korean families, which reflects how well known the resort has become in that market. The Camp Hyatt kids' club runs a full daily programme rather than just supervision, and beach volleyball, kayaking and stand-up paddleboarding all get genuine use rather than sitting unused on a rack. Couples do well here too, especially with a higher-floor ocean view room, but this leans family-resort more than romantic-retreat, and it works best for guests who want several days of straightforward beach life rather than a city-adjacent base.

What We Like

The Tradeoff

The resort sits 12 kilometres from Da Nang's city centre, which means Dragon Bridge, Han Market, the Pink Cathedral and the best street food in the city all require a Grab ride, about 20 minutes and a few dollars each way. That's not a problem for a beach-first trip with the occasional day excursion. It becomes one if you pictured spending evenings wandering the city. Wifi also drops on the balcony and doesn't reach the bathroom, a minor annoyance unless you're trying to work remotely, and the main breakfast restaurant runs real 20-minute waits between 8 and 9:30am. Pools and the beach both get crowded during Vietnamese public holidays, so if quiet matters to you, avoid Tet and the April break.

Location

Location

Non Nuoc Beach sits about 12km south of Da Nang's city centre, in the Ngu Hanh Son district, and 14km from the airport. What that means in practice: this is a beach-holiday base, not a city base. The Marble Mountains are a five-minute taxi ride, close enough to slot in without much planning, but Dragon Bridge, Han Market and the city's best street food are all a 20-minute Grab away. If your trip is built around lounging on a quiet stretch of sand with day trips layered in, the distance works in your favor. If you wanted to walk to dinner most nights, it won't.

Would We Stay Here

YES

The room is beautiful, the beach is beautiful, and the breakfast is excellent enough that leaving means already thinking about which room category to book next time. It isn't the most dramatic hotel in Da Nang, and it isn't the most intimate. It's the one that gets every part of a beach resort holiday right at the same time, which turns out to be rarer than it sounds.

Common Questions

Hyatt Regency Danang Resort and Spa: FAQ

Is Hyatt Regency Danang Resort and Spa worth staying at in Da Nang?

Yes, for most luxury beach-holiday travellers. The Hyatt Regency Danang offers genuine five-star facilities at rates well below comparable properties in Phuket or Bali, with a strong pool complex and reliable service.

How much does Hyatt Regency Danang Resort and Spa cost per night?

Low-season rates start around $180–$220/night. Peak season (December to February) typically runs $300–$450/night for standard rooms.

Where is Hyatt Regency Danang Resort and Spa located in Da Nang?

Located on Non Nuoc Beach in the Ngu Hanh Son district, about 12 km south of Da Nang city centre and 14 km from Da Nang Airport. The Marble Mountains are a short drive away.
