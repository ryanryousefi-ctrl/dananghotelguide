Da Nang's Coffee Scene

Vietnam's third largest city has grown into something of a digital nomad hub over the last decade — laid-back enough to earn the nickname "Chiang Mai by the Sea," comparing it to the Northern Thai city with a spiritual vibe and an excellent coffee scene. You'll find people working on their laptops pretty much everywhere you go in Da Nang.

Vietnam is now the world's second-largest coffee producer, with more than 90% of output being Robusta. Coffee is part of the Vietnamese identity, and Da Nang doesn't shy away from it. You'll find it everywhere: hole-in-the-walls and street carts open until midnight, chic espresso bars with in-house roasters, and home-baked pastries to go alongside.

Below are five cafes that cover the spectrum — tourist-friendly and local, specialty and traditional. Compared to other Vietnamese cities, Da Nang is compact, so getting between these is never more than a short bike ride or taxi.

☕ Specialty Roaster

O2o First Roast

The kind of cafe you'll walk past twice before finding. What it lacks in curb appeal it makes up for in exceptional coffee and an owner who genuinely loves what he's doing.

☕ Specialty
O2o First Roast
📍 18/3b Phan Tứ, Da Nang

O2o feels like a speakeasy: tucked into a residential lane off Da Nang's main thoroughfare, sitting in the first floor of a building that reads more apartment block than coffee shop. If you judge cafes by their facade, you'll walk straight past. Don't. The owner is chatty and loves anime — a fact you'll notice when you flip through the specialty coffee profiles, themed around Japanese animation films. Coffee is medium-to-light roasted, with rotating single-origin Ethiopian heirloom beans and specialty batches from Kenya, Rwanda, and Vietnam. The cold brew with lemon is exceptional: slightly acidic, with floral, orange, and raspberry notes. The matcha with lemon and gin is quietly excellent too.

$$  ·  Specialty from ~$3

📍 Getting there

O2o is in one of the leafy side streets off Da Nang's main drag. It's a small space — a sofa by the counter, a handful of seats — so arriving off-peak is worth it. No dedicated signage from the street.

☕ In-House Roaster

Puna Coffee

Rustic, warm, and entirely Vietnamese-owned — the kind of place where you settle in for two hours and forget you had somewhere to be.

☕ In-House Roaster
Puna Coffee
📍 132 Le Quang Dao, Da Nang

Puna sits smack in the middle of Da Nang's tourist buzz — sharing the neighbourhood with design-led cafes, local food joints, bakeries, and vintage thrift shops — but it earns its place. The interiors are rustic wood and terracotta, the tablecloths are floral, and the air smells correctly of coffee. They roast in-house, with beans from Colombia, Ethiopia, Kenya, and Vietnam's northern and central mountains, plus an Ethiopian and Vietnamese specialty blend that delivers a genuinely complex mix of chocolate, plum, and caramel. Some bags are available to buy from the front shelves. Beyond coffee: oolong tea sets, pineapple cookie sandwiches, fresh-from-the-oven carrot cake, and a solid all-day breakfast menu of pancakes, eggs benedict, and smoothie bowls. The outdoor seats are excellent for people-watching. As one of the baristas put it, despite Vietnam's reputation for Robusta, "many young people now go for Arabica."

$  ·  Coffee from ~$2

☕ Hai Chau District

Indigo

Across the Han River from the tourist strip — where the locals actually go — you'll find one of Da Nang's most charming cafes, right opposite a pink cathedral.

Indigo's bar — illustrated art tiles, an Eureka Helios 75, and a bamboo bike mounted on the wall

☕ Hai Chau District
Indigo
📍 177 Tran Phu, Hai Chau 1, Da Nang

Not many tourists — and even most remote workers — make it to the Hai Chau District. That's worth fixing. This part of the city has an eclectic mix of pizzerias, coffee shops, and 6am breakfast spots where Vietnamese aunties serve bò né off sizzling cast-iron plates. To get here you'll cross one of the bridges over the Han River, and a couple of blocks from the riverside walking path you'll find Indigo — housed in the same building as M Village Hotel, right opposite the city's pink cathedral with stained-glass windows and only a short walk from Han Market. The focus is on creating a warm, cozy space: cushy couches, board games, a tiny boutique selling handmade bags and Indigo-branded notebooks. The brew station hosts a black Eureka Helios 75 and a full V60 setup. Try the tangerine-infused Americano, or the iced coconut coffee.

$  ·  Coffee from ~$2
Follow on Instagram →
📍 Getting there

Indigo is in Hai Chau 1, across the Han River from the beach strip. Walk or ride a bike over any of the central bridges — the riverside path is pleasant on foot. Han Market is a two-minute walk away, which makes this a natural pairing.

☕ Award-Winning

Àla

Serious about coffee in a way that can feel rare — awards on the walls, tasting note cards with every cup, and a Panama Gesha on a La Marzocco that will recalibrate your expectations for the city.

☕ Award-Winning
Àla
📍 27, Alley 113 Nguyễn Chí Thanh, Da Nang

Àla feels like coming home after a long day — except you're sitting next to Vietnamese students and tech workers who are busy on their laptops. The two-story cafe has a rooftop and sits in a tropical garden, with clean white interiors, wall creepers, and a main floor bar where sunlight filters through glass roof panes. Coffee is excellent and taken seriously: every cup comes with tasting note cards, the awards are proudly displayed, and the espresso machine is an eye-catching orange La Marzocco. On a rainy-day visit, the Panama Gesha espresso was wonderfully clean and bright — floral, grape, tangerine. The roast list runs from light to dark, sourced from Ethiopia, Honduras, Brazil, and beyond. The glass cabinet of baked goods is worth attention, and from time to time Àla hosts cupping and brewing sessions — worth checking their socials.

$$  ·  Specialty from ~$3.50

☕ My Khe Beach Area

XLIII Specialty Coffee

The most expensive coffee bar in Da Nang — and one that justifies every dong. Minimalist, industrial, and staffed by people who actually know what they're talking about.

XLIII's bar — a full pour-over station, exposed brick, and white-oak stools on a polished concrete counter

☕ Specialty — Multi-Location
XLIII Specialty Coffee
📍 422 Ngo Thi Si, My An, Ngu Hanh Son, Da Nang

Only a few blocks from Puna on Da Nang's bustling seaside strip, but the two couldn't be more different. XLIII is modern and industrial — the kind of place where the staff walks you through every step, from picking your roast to choosing your brewing method (machine-drip, pour-over, or milk-based), explaining how each approach shifts the flavor profile of the blend. The roast list focuses on origins: Nueva Alianza Red Gesha from Peru, subtle and citrus-sweet with jasmine; Sudan Rume from Colombian producers; Volcan Valley from the slopes of Panama's Baru Volcano. The food holds up too — sourdough croissants, buttery madeleines, parma ham sandwiches. The outside seating wraps around greenery and koi ponds, which makes it an ideal spot for a slow evening. XLIII started here in Da Nang and has since grown to four outlets across Vietnam, two of them in Ho Chi Minh City.

$$$  ·  Specialty from ~$5
Follow on Instagram →
💡 Editor's note

XLIII is the most polished specialty coffee experience in Da Nang — but it's also genuinely the most expensive. Budget travellers and digital nomads looking for an all-day work spot will be more comfortable at Puna or Indigo. XLIII is best for a focused hour with excellent coffee, not a six-hour laptop session.
