Where to actually shop in Da Nang, from Han Market souvenirs to malls to independent boutiques.

Han Market and Con Market - Fabric, Silk, and Souvenirs

Han Market

Han Market (Cho Han) on the Han River is Da Nang's most tourist-friendly covered market. The upper floors are where you'll find silk, ao dai, tailored clothing, lacquerware, local coffee, and the usual souvenirs.

The ground floor is fresh produce and seafood. Worth a walk-through for the sights, but it's not where most visitors are actually shopping.

For silk, feel the fabric before you buy. The real thing feels cool to the touch, has a natural sheen that shifts as the light hits it, and is light in your hand.

Polyester sold as silk (or blended with it) runs heavier, warmer, and doesn't have that same shifting sheen. A 100% silk scarf at Han Market runs 200,000-500,000 VND ($8-20), depending on quality and how well you negotiate.

Con Market

Con Market (Cho Con) is bigger and rougher around the edges than Han Market: more local, less built for tourists. Its fabric section is genuinely excellent for raw fabric and lengths you can take straight to a tailor.

Prices per meter run well below the tourist-facing fabric shops. If you've got a specific tailoring project in mind, buy the fabric here and take it to a tailor in the same area.

Con Market also has good prices on shoes, bags, and everyday clothing that isn't aimed at tourists at all. It takes more navigating, but patience pays off in better prices.

Market hours: Han Market runs 6am-6pm. Con Market runs 6am-7pm.

Both are busiest mid-morning on weekdays. Get there before 4pm for the best selection and the most patient vendors - in that last hour before closing, stalls start packing up and lose interest in new customers.

Custom Tailoring in Da Nang

Da Nang has working tailors, mostly clustered around Han Market and Con Market. Custom tailoring here can genuinely produce excellent results at a fraction of home-country prices.

The catch is time. You need enough of it, and realistic expectations about the process.

The Tailoring Process

Step 1: choose your fabric, either at the market itself or from the tailor's own stock.

Don't rush this part. Fabric choice shapes the final result, and getting talked into a bad one under time pressure is the most common regret.

Step 2: talk through the design and agree on it. Bring reference photos if you have a specific look in mind.

Be precise about cut, collar style, sleeve length, and fit. If there's a language barrier, Google Translate helps, but a photo communicates more cleanly than a description ever will.

Step 3: first fitting, usually 1-2 days after you're measured.

This is where adjustments get made. Don't skip it. Fixing things after the garment is finished is much harder.

Step 4: final pickup, another 1-2 days later.

Budget a minimum of 3-4 days total for the whole process. More is better.

What Can Be Tailored

Ao dai (the traditional Vietnamese dress) is the most requested item. Expect 300,000-700,000 VND ($12-28) for labor, plus 200,000-500,000 VND for decent fabric.

A simple dress shirt or blouse runs a similar range. More complex Western pieces like suits are possible at some tailors, but results vary. Hoi An's tailors have more experience with Western suit construction.

Tailoring reality check: With only 2 days in Da Nang, tailoring is a gamble. There isn't enough time for fittings and fixes. If you want it done right, set aside at least 4-5 days, or save it for Hoi An, where the entire old town runs on the tailoring trade.

Rushed tailoring disappoints more often than not.

Shopping Malls in Da Nang

Vincom Plaza Da Nang

Vincom Plaza is Da Nang's main mall, sitting centrally in the city. It's a mix of Vietnamese and international fashion brands, a hypermarket, food court, cinema, and the usual mall trimmings.

It's air-conditioned, which matters more than it sounds like on a hot day. The fashion selection covers mid-range Vietnamese brands alongside names you'll recognize from home.

Prices are fixed. No negotiating here, and none expected.

Vincom is worth a stop for packaged food and drink to bring home (the supermarket section carries local coffee, dried fruit, and snacks at decent prices), replacing anything you forgot to pack, or just killing a hot afternoon indoors.

Lotte Mart Da Nang

Lotte Mart pairs retail with one of the better-stocked supermarkets in the city. That supermarket is the real draw for local food products: packaged pho kits, instant coffee, dried seafood, local sauces, and snacks, all at prices well below tourist shops or the airport.

Parkson Da Nang

A smaller mid-range mall with fashion and lifestyle brands. It doesn't match Vincom for range, but it's a fine stop if you're already in that part of the city.

Stronger on local Vietnamese fashion brands than international names.

Vietnamese Coffee to Bring Home

Coffee is genuinely the best value item you'll bring back from Da Nang. The quality is excellent, it weighs nothing, and the price per kilogram is a fraction of what specialty coffee costs in Western countries.

What to Buy

Trung Nguyen Legend is the most recognized brand and the safest pick, whether it's a gift or for yourself. Their roasted and ground coffee comes in numbered strengths (1-5, with 5 the strongest and most robusta-heavy).

The G7 instant sachets travel well and make a legitimate Vietnamese iced coffee with just hot water and ice. Grab a box if you want to recreate ca phe sua da at home.

Highlands Coffee sells packaged beans and ground coffee at their stores and in supermarkets. Their robusta-arabica blend works well for traditional Vietnamese coffee brewed through a phin filter.

Weasel coffee (civet coffee, kopi luwak) costs a premium. Only buy it from an established retailer if you're going to bother, since fakes are common and the gap in quality between real and imitation is significant.

Expect 200,000-500,000+ VND per 100g for the genuine article.

Where to Buy

Big C, Lotte Mart, and Winmart supermarkets have the best prices. Market prices run similar.

Tourist-facing coffee shops charge a markup, and the airport charges the most of all.

Bring an extra bag or leave room in your luggage. Coffee is one of the few things actually worth overpacking for.

Phin Filter

If you want to make Vietnamese coffee properly once you're home, buy a phin (the traditional metal drip filter). They run 30,000-80,000 VND ($1.20-3.20) at markets and supermarkets.

Small, light, and the only way to actually get ca phe sua da right.

What to Avoid Buying in Da Nang

Tourist Trap Items

Mass-produced "traditional" souvenirs with no real craft behind them: identical across every city in Vietnam and often made in China. Fake branded goods (sunglasses, watches, bags, clothing) are another skip. Customs enforcement in plenty of countries targets these, and the quality is consistently bad anyway.

Same with "Vietnamese jade" or gemstones sold off market stalls with no certification. There's no way to verify provenance or quality on the spot.

Overpriced Airport Purchases

Da Nang airport retail marks up everything, including local coffee, lacquerware, and standard souvenirs. If you haven't shopped by the time you're there, it's fine as a last resort, but you're paying 30-50% above market or supermarket rates for the same products.

Seafood and Perishables

Fresh seafood isn't going anywhere with you unless it's vacuum-sealed and dried. The dried squid snacks (muc kho) are legitimate and travel fine, but anything fresh or only lightly preserved isn't worth the spoilage risk, let alone the biosecurity issues at customs.

Local Perspective

Ryan's Honest Take on Shopping in Da Nang

Every time friends visit, I take them to Lotte Mart to load up on Trung Nguyen coffee. It's the best shopping value in the city, full stop: high quality, cheap compared to home, and something people actually use once they're back.

It also makes a far better gift than a generic souvenir.

For tailoring, my honest advice is to skip Da Nang and do it in Hoi An if you have the option. The tailor scene in Hoi An's old town is better organized, has more experience with international customers, and offers more fabric variety.

Da Nang's tailors are competent, but it isn't their specialty the way it is in Hoi An.

Where Da Nang genuinely delivers is local produce. The supermarkets and markets stock excellent dried seafood, fresh fruit, packaged snacks, and condiments that make great gifts and are hard to find back home. Spend less time at the souvenir stalls and more at Lotte Mart.

You'll thank me later.

Common Questions

Da Nang Shopping: FAQ

What is the best thing to buy in Da Nang?+

Vietnamese coffee is the single best value purchase you can make. Trung Nguyen Legend and G7 instant coffee packs are excellent quality for a fraction of specialty cafe prices back home.

Buy at supermarkets (Lotte Mart, Big C) for the best prices. Other purchases worth making: silk fabric or scarves (test the material first), custom-tailored ao dai if your schedule allows fittings, and lacquerware from a reputable source.

Is Da Nang good for shopping?+

It's decent, not exceptional. For markets, tailoring, and craft quality, Hoi An (45 minutes away) is significantly better and worth a day trip on its own.

Da Nang is solid for local food products at supermarkets, everyday shopping at malls, and picking up anything you forgot to pack. If shopping is a real priority for your trip, give Hoi An the day instead.

Where can I get clothes tailored in Da Nang?+

Tailors cluster around Han Market and Con Market. Plan on 3-5 days minimum for fabric selection, measurement, fitting, and pickup.

An ao dai runs roughly 300,000-700,000 VND ($12-28) in labor plus fabric cost. For stronger tailoring and more English-language experience, Hoi An's tailor scene is more developed and easier for visitors to navigate.

What Vietnamese coffee should I bring home?+

Trung Nguyen Legend for whole or ground coffee, G7 sachets for instant Vietnamese iced coffee. Highlands Coffee's packaged beans are also good.

Buy at Lotte Mart, Big C, or Winmart for the best prices - same products as the tourist shops, 30-50% cheaper. Grab a phin (metal drip filter, 30,000-80,000 VND) too, so you can make it properly once you're home.

Does Da Nang have shopping malls?+

Yes. Vincom Plaza is the main one, with fashion brands, a supermarket, cinema, and food court.

Lotte Mart has an excellent supermarket section for local food products to bring home. Parkson is a smaller mall option.

All three are useful for air-conditioned shopping, familiar brands, and stocking up on local food at better prices than the tourist shops charge.

Is silk at Da Nang markets genuine?+

Some is, some is polyester or a blend sold as silk. Test before you buy: real silk feels cool and smooth, has a subtle sheen that shifts with the light, and is light in your hand.

Blended or synthetic fabric feels warmer and heavier. The burn test (a thread should smell like burnt hair, not plastic) is the definitive check, but it's not exactly practical mid-negotiation.

Buy from vendors who'll let you handle the fabric and who'll take it back if the quality isn't what they claimed.
