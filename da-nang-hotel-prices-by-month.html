About this index

Da Nang hotel prices move on four separate forces, and they don't line up into one tidy seasonal curve: the summer beach season (May–August), when Vietnamese domestic demand peaks; the rainy season (September–November), which pushes prices down hard; the Tet holiday spike (late January to mid-February), which produces the highest rates of the year; and growing Korean inbound tourism, which has created a second demand peak in April that simply wasn't there three years ago. Knowing which force is driving a given month is what separates overpaying by 50% from booking well.

The index below was compiled by manually sampling rates across 20 representative properties across Da Nang's three main hotel tiers: 3-star city hotels, 4-star beach hotels, and 5-star resort properties. We cross-referenced those against booking platform data from late 2025 through January 2026. This isn't pulled from an automated feed.

It's an editorially curated index built to give travelers a planning baseline they can actually trust.

ℹ All prices are in USD and reflect average nightly rates for a standard double room, excluding taxes and resort fees (typically 10–20% on top of quoted rates). Figures represent market midpoints, not outlier flash deals or peak rack rates. Expect actual prices to vary by property, lead time, and booking channel.

What These Numbers Mean in Practice

Da Nang's seasonal price swing is steeper than most Southeast Asian beach destinations. A 5-star resort averaging $260/night in peak summer can genuinely be booked for $100–$130 in October: same property, same facilities, less than half the cost.

That swing comes down to the hard stop of rainy season. Most leisure travelers won't schedule a Da Nang trip in October no matter how cheap it gets, so properties have to discount hard to fill rooms.

For budget travelers, the low-season 3-star market at $22–$28/night puts Da Nang in the same range as Chiang Mai or Canggu (our cheap hotels in Da Nang guide lists specific options by area). For anyone looking to upgrade, low-season 5-star pricing is about as good a deal as you'll find in the country.

What's driving the change?

Korean inbound tourism has been the single largest demand-side factor reshaping Da Nang's hotel market since 2023. Direct routes from Seoul, Busan, and Jeju expanded substantially, and Korean travelers, who lean heavily toward 4-star and above properties, have created a second demand peak in April that has lifted mid-tier pricing year-round.

International recovery from post-pandemic lows continued through 2024–2025, with European and Australian arrivals returning to near pre-2020 levels. That broad recovery supported pricing across all categories, without the supply constraints that would otherwise cap how far rates can climb.

Rising operating costs, energy, labour, F&B, pushed minimum viable nightly rates up too. The roughly 16–19% cumulative increase in the 3-star and 4-star segments tracks closely with Vietnam's hospitality sector cost inflation over the same stretch.

Expanding 5-star supply has partly offset luxury price growth. New resort openings along Non Nuoc Beach between 2023 and 2025 added room inventory, which kept rate growth in the top tier to just 8% over three years, the most restrained of any segment.

More properties in the pipeline are expected to open in 2026.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Da Nang expensive compared to Phuket or Bali?

Da Nang runs noticeably cheaper than both Phuket and Bali across every hotel category. A comparable 4-star beachfront property that costs $150–$200/night in Phuket or $130–$180 in Bali's Seminyak typically runs $70–$110 in Da Nang during peak season.

Five-star resorts follow the same pattern. Da Nang's luxury tier averages $200–$350 at peak, while equivalent Phuket properties regularly exceed $400–$600. Food and transport cost less here too, so the overall trip cost gap ends up wider than accommodation alone suggests.

When are hotels cheapest in Da Nang?

October and November are Da Nang's real low season. Three-star hotels average $22–$28/night, 4-star properties drop to $45–$58, and 5-star resorts are available for $90–$130, roughly 55–60% below peak rates.

The trade-off is weather. Da Nang's rainy season peaks through mid-October to mid-November, with the heaviest rainfall of the year. September is also low season but wetter still.

If dry weather matters more to you than the rock-bottom price, late November or early December gets you rising prices but noticeably better beach and outdoor conditions.

How far in advance should I book Da Nang hotels?

Booking windows shift sharply by season. For Tet (late January/February) and peak summer (June–August), book 6–10 weeks out. Quality properties at mid-range prices sell out fast, and rates spike once lead time gets short.

For shoulder months (March, April, December), 3–4 weeks is enough. During low season (September–November), booking last-minute is fine and sometimes pays off, since properties discount hard to fill rooms.

For 5-star resorts during any peak period, book early. Pool villas and promotional packages have limited availability and rarely get discounted on short notice.

Are 5-star resorts worth it in Da Nang?

Da Nang's 5-star resorts offer some of the strongest luxury-for-money in Southeast Asia. Properties along My Khe and Non Nuoc Beach, including internationally branded resorts, deliver private beach access, full-service spas, multiple dining outlets, and large rooms at prices that would only buy a mid-range city hotel in Western capitals.

The clearest value window is shoulder season: late March, April, or December, when rates sit 35–45% below peak with full operations and favourable weather. Even peak season rates of $200–$350 hold up well given the service level, setting, and inclusions.
