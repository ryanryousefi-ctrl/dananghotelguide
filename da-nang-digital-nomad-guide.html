Digital Nomad Guide — Da Nang — 2026

Working Remotely in Da Nang

Where to live, where to work, what the visa situation actually looks like, and the things nobody mentions.

Updated 2026 · Local editor

The Short Version

Da Nang works for digital nomads — but it's not Chiang Mai or Bali. An Thuong is the right base: walkable to the beach, good WiFi cafes, decent food and bars, manageable cost of living. Internet is fast and reliable. The community is small and you'll build your own social circle. The 90-day e-visa is reasonably flexible. Visa runs are the main inconvenience for longer stays. If you want a large pre-existing nomad community, look elsewhere. If you want a proper beach city with affordable living and you're comfortable building your own routine, Da Nang is worth it.

Editor's Pick

An Thuong Is the Right Base

The An Thuong street grid — roughly the area between Tran Bich San and Ho Xuan Huong, running back from the My Khe beachfront — has the best concentration of what nomads need. WiFi cafes, Western-friendly bars, a 7-minute walk to the beach, and apartments that cost a fraction of what you'd pay in Bangkok. Don't base yourself at the beachfront (overpriced resorts) or in the city centre across the Han River (worse cafes, less useful for beach access).

Da Nang Signature Pick

Work at a Cafe, Swim at Noon

The premise of Da Nang for nomads is real: work from a cafe in An Thuong from 8am, walk to My Khe at noon, back at your desk by 1:30pm. It functions. The beach is genuinely clean and not overcrowded in the mornings, the cafe WiFi holds up, and the cost of doing this daily is low. The swim break is not a fantasy — it's a reliable part of the routine here.

Where to live

Best Neighbourhoods to Base Yourself

Best for Nomads

An Thuong

Expat-friendly neighbourhood behind My Khe beach. Highest concentration of WiFi cafes, Western restaurants, and bars. Apartments available for 8-18 million VND/month ($320-720). Short-term rentals via Airbnb common here.

Best overall base for digital nomads
Beach Access

My Khe Beachfront

On Vo Nguyen Giap, the main beach road. Great views, but mostly resort hotels. Fewer local cafes, higher prices. Reasonable for monthly hotel bookings but apartment options are limited and pricier.

Good for short stays, expensive for longer
City Feel

Han River Area

Da Nang's urban core, across the river from Son Tra. More Vietnamese city character. Closer to Con Market, Cham Museum. Good for those who want city life over beach access. Fewer nomad-facing cafes.

Better if you don't prioritise beach access
Local Pace

Hoa Cuong / Cam Le

South of centre. Almost entirely residential and local-facing. Cheapest rents (6-10 million VND/month). Very few cafes with good WiFi. Good Vietnamese language skills or strong tolerance for language barriers required.

For experienced Vietnam travellers on a tight budget
Where to work

Best Cafes for Getting Work Done

  • An Thuong area cafes (general)
    Multiple cafes along Tran Bich San and Ngu Hanh Son streets. Most have password-protected WiFi, laptop-friendly seating, and all-day service. Ask for the WiFi password when ordering.
    50-150 Mbps typical
  • Con market-area coffee shops
    Cheaper coffee, basic seating. Less optimal for all-day working — smaller tables, sometimes smoky. Good for a quick session and strong ca phe sua da.
    20-80 Mbps
  • Hotel lobby cafes
    Several mid-range hotels on My Khe open their lobby cafes to non-guests. Good AC, usually fast WiFi, quieter. Prices are 20-30% higher than street cafes.
    Usually 100+ Mbps
Coworking spaces note: Da Nang has a small number of dedicated coworking spaces but fewer options than Hanoi or Ho Chi Minh City. Most nomads work from cafes rather than coworking spaces. If you need a dedicated desk, private call space, or mailing address, research current coworking options closer to your arrival date — the scene changes quickly.

Visa

The Visa Situation

Vietnam's e-visa (90 days, single or multiple entry) covers most nationalities and is the standard starting point. It's applied for online, costs around $25 USD, and processes in a few days. The 90-day window is enough for most nomads to settle in and assess whether they want to stay longer.

For longer stays, the main option is a visa run — leaving Vietnam and re-entering, usually by flying to Thailand, Cambodia, or Laos. A budget visa run to Bangkok costs $100-200 all-in. Some nationalities can do border crossings more cheaply. The cadence most nomads settle into is one visa run every 60-90 days.

Always verify current visa rules for your nationality before making plans — Vietnam's visa policy changes, and this guide reflects general 2026 conditions. Nationalities with visa exemptions (15-45 days depending on country) should confirm current exemption status.
Honest downsides

What Doesn't Work

  • The nomad community is small. Da Nang has a resident expat scene (mostly retirees, English teachers, and a smaller number of remote workers), but it's not Chiang Mai or Bali. Meeting other nomads takes effort. You'll build your own social circle rather than drop into an existing one.
  • Monsoon season is real. October and November are genuinely unpleasant. Flooding, multiple-day rain events, typhoon risk. If you're on a monthly lease that runs through October or November, have a plan — either commit to staying and working indoors, or time your visa run to skip those months.
  • Vietnamese is genuinely difficult. Unlike Thailand, basic English is less common outside of tourist-facing businesses. Ordering at local restaurants, dealing with landlords, or navigating any official process requires either Vietnamese, a local contact who can help, or high tolerance for trial and error.
  • Visa logistics are a mild constant overhead. There's no digital nomad visa in Vietnam yet. Every 90 days, you're planning and executing a visa run. It's manageable — but it's a recurring task that Thailand, Malaysia, and some other countries don't require.
  • Limited nightlife variety. An Thuong has a handful of good bars. The city's nightlife is mostly resort-beach or karaoke. If a strong, diverse nightlife scene is part of why you chose this lifestyle, Da Nang undershoots. Hoi An is quieter still. HCMC is the right answer for nightlife.

Common Questions

Is Da Nang good for digital nomads?
Da Nang works well for digital nomads with reasonable expectations. An Thuong has a cluster of good WiFi cafes within walking distance of My Khe Beach. Cost of living is low, food is excellent, and the city is easy to navigate. Downsides: visa logistics are more complex than Thailand, coworking options are limited, and the nomad social scene is smaller than Chiang Mai or Bali.
What is the cost of living in Da Nang for digital nomads?
A comfortable month in Da Nang — private apartment, restaurant meals, Grab transport, cafe work budget — runs $600-1,050 USD. Add $300-500 for a more social lifestyle with bars and activities. This is lower than Bali or Bangkok for comparable quality.
What visa options are there for digital nomads in Vietnam?
Vietnam's 90-day e-visa is the standard starting point for most nationalities. It can be extended by doing a visa run — leaving and re-entering Vietnam. A budget visa run to Thailand or Cambodia costs $100-200 all-in. Vietnam does not have an official digital nomad visa. Always verify current visa rules for your nationality before booking.
Where is the best area to live in Da Nang as a digital nomad?
An Thuong neighbourhood — the grid of streets behind My Khe Beach — is the best base for nomads. It has the highest concentration of WiFi cafes and Western-friendly restaurants, walkable beach access, and lower rents than the beachfront strip. Apartments run 8-14 million VND/month ($320-560).
Does Da Nang have good internet speed?
Yes. Vietnam has some of Southeast Asia's best internet infrastructure. Home fiber runs 50-100 Mbps and costs almost nothing to add to a lease. Cafe WiFi in An Thuong is typically 50-150 Mbps. A Viettel or Vietnamobile mobile SIM provides fast, cheap data as a backup — 10GB for around $4-5.
