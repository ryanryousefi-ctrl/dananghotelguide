Overview

What to know before you book

Honestly? Da Nang is one of the easiest cities in Southeast Asia to travel with kids. The beaches are calm, the food is cheap and good, taxis are everywhere, and the resorts here are genuinely set up for families — not just "we have a kids menu" family-friendly, but actual pools, actual waterparks, actual activities.

The three picks below cover different situations. If your kids are the type who could spend three days in a waterpark and never ask to leave, Mikazuki is the obvious answer and worth planning your whole trip around. If you want a chill beach base without spending a lot, Four Points is solid. And if you want to be right in the middle of everything — restaurants, the beach strip, easy Grab rides — Muong Thanh puts you there at a price that won't hurt.

None of these are obscure. Locals recommend all three regularly. They're just not always the ones that show up first on travel blogs.

💡 Local tip

If Mikazuki is in the budget, plan at least two nights. One day isn't enough to get through everything — especially with younger kids who take time to warm up to the slides. Weekdays are noticeably quieter than weekends.

Best for Families · Full Waterpark

Da Nang Mikazuki Japanese Resorts & Spa

This is the one. If you have kids who are old enough to go on slides, just book this and don't overthink it. Mikazuki has an enormous indoor-outdoor waterpark — heated indoor pools, outdoor slides, a lazy river, arcades, a wave pool, the works. It's operated by a Japanese company so everything is clean, organised, and well-run. Most families I know who've stayed here say their kids refused to leave.

The catch: it's about 25 minutes south of the main My Khe Beach strip, past the Marble Mountains. You're not walking to restaurants at night. That's a real consideration. But honestly, you won't need to — the resort has plenty of dining on-site, and for a 2-day staycation focused entirely on the kids having the time of their lives, the location doesn't matter at all. If you can go on a Tuesday or Wednesday, do it. Weekends get loud and crowded fast.

The best waterpark resort in Da Nang by a significant margin. Indoor and outdoor pools, multiple slides, a lazy river, wave pool, arcade games — kids genuinely don't want to leave. Good Value · Quiet Beach End · My Khe

Four Points by Sheraton Danang

Four Points sits on the quieter, less crowded northern stretch of My Khe Beach — away from the bar noise and the weekend crowds that build up around the Dragon Bridge end. The pool is genuinely good, the beach access is direct, and the rooms are clean and spacious without anything to complain about. It's a Sheraton property, so the quality floor is reliable.

This is the pick if you want a comfortable beach holiday at a price that doesn't require a second mortgage. Nothing flashy, nothing missing. You'll spend your days at the pool or the beach, eat well at the nearby restaurants, and pay a reasonable amount for the privilege. For families who don't need a waterpark and just want a solid base near the ocean, this is the sensible call.

Best Location · Right in the Action · My Khe

Muong Thanh Luxury Da Nang Hotel

Muong Thanh is the answer when location is the priority. It sits directly across the street from the beach on the main My Khe strip — you walk out the lobby and you're 60 seconds from the sand. Restaurants, cafés, the evening seafood spots, everything is within walking distance. The infinity pool on the upper floors has a genuinely good view, and the breakfast buffet is the kind of spread that keeps kids happy and parents from having to think about the first meal of the day.

Muong Thanh is a Vietnamese hotel chain, which means the price is better than equivalent international brands and the service has a local warmth that some of the big foreign chains don't quite match. It's consistently one of the better-value options on the whole strip. For families who want to be in the middle of everything — grab food easily, reach the beach in two minutes, and not spend a fortune — this is the pick.

FAQ

Family Hotels Da Nang — FAQs

Is Mikazuki worth it if we're only in Da Nang for a few days?
If you have kids who love water attractions, yes — but only if you stay two nights minimum. One day at Mikazuki feels rushed and the resort is 25 minutes from the main tourist area, so spending one night and then moving on doesn't really make sense logistically. If you're short on time and want to see more of the city, Four Points or Muong Thanh are better picks.
What's the best day to visit Mikazuki's waterpark?
Weekdays, full stop. Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday are the quietest. Weekends — especially Saturday — fill up fast with Vietnamese families from Da Nang and tour groups from nearby resorts. The difference in crowd levels is significant. If your schedule allows it, plan your Mikazuki nights around a weekday stay.
Is Muong Thanh actually on the beach?
It's across the street from the beach — not beachfront, but the walk is genuinely about 60 seconds. The beach access is free and easy. The trade-off is that the views from the pool and upper floors are better than most actually-on-the-sand hotels because of the elevated position. For most families, this is a non-issue.
How far is My Khe Beach from Ba Na Hills?
About 30–40 minutes by Grab or taxi, depending on traffic. Most families do Ba Na Hills as a day trip from whichever beach hotel they're in. There's no real reason to stay near Ba Na Hills itself — the resort hotels up there are overpriced for what they are. Stay on the beach and Grab up for the day.
Does Muong Thanh's breakfast buffet actually feed kids well?
Yes. It's a full spread — eggs cooked to order, Vietnamese noodles, Western options, fruit, pastries. It's not a token "continental breakfast" situation. Most families find it genuinely covers the first meal of the day so well that lunch ends up being light. It's one of the main reasons locals recommend Muong Thanh over some pricier competitors.
Is Da Nang safe for kids to swim at the beach?
Generally yes, between February and September. The water is calm, the beaches are wide, and there are lifeguards at the main My Khe stretch during peak hours. October and November bring typhoon-season swells that make swimming risky — the red flag system is enforced, and you should take it seriously. Outside those months, the beach is very kid-friendly.
