Melia Danang Beach Resort

“Only 109 rooms and a pool design that shows up in architecture magazines.”

Our Take

The Melia Danang photographs well, all-white facade, dramatic vertical gardens running down the building, a pool complex that does exactly what a pool on Non Nuoc Beach is supposed to do.

What you find on arrival is that it also looks good in person, which isn't always true of hotels that have been photographed to death on Instagram.

The scale is right, too.

With only 109 rooms, the resort doesn't overwhelm itself: the pools don't feel industrial, the beach service is attentive, and the staff-to-guest ratio stays in a place where people actually notice you.

The Level programme deserves more than a footnote.

Suite guests get a private dedicated reception (no main lobby check-in), an exclusive lounge with afternoon snack buffets, twice-daily housekeeping, and small touches like cold soft drinks sent down to the pool without being asked.

Guest experiences vary, though. Multiple travelers call it the highlight of their stay; one UK traveler who had a difficult run advises people to avoid it.

The truth is probably in between: the Level experience depends partly on which staff are on duty that day.

When it's good, it's notably good.

The Rooms

The Rooms

Entry-level Deluxe Sea View rooms run around 55 square metres with large balconies over the pool and ocean, generous for the category. The design is contemporary with Spanish touches, terracotta accents, plant walls, a color palette that's warm rather than clinical. Bathrooms have rainfall showers and freestanding tubs in the higher categories. These are proper rooms, not just boxes with a view.

The one recurring complaint worth knowing before you book: soundproofing between rooms. It's come up consistently enough across guest reviews, not universal, but frequent enough to plan around if you're a light sleeper.

Which room would we choose? A Deluxe Sea View room for most stays, the balcony and pool view justify the modest premium. If the budget stretches, book into the Level for the private check-in and lounge access, it's the more memorable version of this hotel.

The Reason to Book It

The Infinity Pool

A long infinity pool with ocean views, a family pool, and an adults-only pool make up the pool complex, and the design is genuinely striking, this is the pool that shows up in architecture magazines and travel photography. It's maintained to a high standard and rarely overcrowded given the small room count.

Loungers do run short at peak times, so stake a claim early if you're not in the Level program. Small detail, good detail: the Level lounge sends cold drinks down to the pool without being asked.

Pool + Beach

Pool + Beach

Non Nuoc Beach itself is quieter and cleaner than the main My Khe strip further north, which is either exactly what you want from a resort stay or a reason to look elsewhere if you want beach-town buzz. The Marble Mountains are five minutes away if you want a break from the pool. Between the three pools and the beach, the property doesn't feel like it's competing with itself for space, a genuine benefit of keeping the room count at 109.

Food + Breakfast

Food + Drink

El Meson runs Spanish-influenced Mediterranean and Vietnamese cuisine in an open-sided setting, giving the Melia a more distinctive dining identity than most Da Nang resorts. The breakfast buffet has genuinely good variety, guests specifically call out the salads at breakfast, which sounds odd until you eat them.

Book at least one dinner at El Meson rather than defaulting to room service, the Spanish menu is different enough from the standard resort buffet rotation to be worth the reservation. Hoi An is 30 minutes south by car if you want a night out beyond the property.

Who It's For

Families, Couples & Who It Suits

This works best for couples and design-conscious travelers who want a five-star resort without the scale and noise of the bigger My Khe properties. Melia loyalty members get real added value here. Families are catered for, the family pool and generous room sizes work fine, but the adults-only pool and the Level programme's quieter register suggest this property leans couples-first. Families wanting more dedicated kids' programming would be better served by the Hyatt Regency or Sheraton Grand.

What We Like

What We Like

Only 109 rooms

Keeps the pools and beach from ever feeling industrial, a genuine rarity at the five-star level in Da Nang.

The infinity pool

Among the most striking pool designs in Da Nang, and it rarely feels crowded given the small guest count.

Non Nuoc Beach

Calmer and cleaner than the My Khe strip, a real advantage if you want quiet over buzz.

The Level programme

Private check-in, lounge access, and small touches that genuinely elevate the stay when the staffing is right.

El Meson's Spanish menu

A distinct culinary identity that stands apart from the generic resort buffet rotation.

The Tradeoff

Soundproofing between rooms is a persistent, recurring complaint, guests report hearing neighbors clearly enough to disrupt sleep, and it's come up consistently enough across reviews that it's worth planning around. Ask for a room away from connecting walls if you're a light sleeper. Sunloungers run short around the pool at busy times if you're not in the Level programme, and the spa and gym haven't been updated to match the rest of the property. The city is 13km north, roughly 25 minutes by taxi, so costs add up if you're heading into central Da Nang every day.

Location

Location

Non Nuoc Beach in the Ngu Hanh Son district, about 13km south of the city centre and 15km from the airport. This is quieter, less commercial coastline than the main My Khe strip, no beach bars stacked one after another, fewer restaurants immediately outside the gate. What that means in practice: you're relying on the resort for most evenings, with the Marble Mountains five minutes away for a break, and Hoi An 30 minutes south if you want a full day out. If your idea of a Da Nang holiday includes wandering into a different restaurant every night, budget for taxis. If you want the resort itself to be the destination, the quiet works in your favor.

Would We Stay Here

YES

In a Level suite, without hesitation. The combination of the small-resort scale, the pool design, the Non Nuoc Beach setting, and what the Level programme delivers at its best makes this one of the more considered choices in Da Nang's mid-luxury tier. It's not trying to be the InterContinental, it sits in a different price band and a different mode. On its own terms, it's very good.

Common Questions

Melia Danang Beach Resort: FAQ

Is Melia Danang Beach Resort worth staying at in Da Nang?

Yes, a beautifully designed resort with one of Da Nang's most impressive pool setups and a quieter beach than the My Khe strip. Strong value for a five-star property.

How much does Melia Danang Beach Resort cost per night?

Low season rates start around $170–$220/night. Peak season rises to $300–$450/night.

Where is Melia Danang Beach Resort located in Da Nang?

On Non Nuoc Beach in the Ngu Hanh Son district, about 13 km south of Da Nang city and 15 km from Da Nang Airport.
