I've been living in Da Nang long enough to know which hotels mean it and which ones don't. The ones that mean it show up in the details — the weight of the towels, the way the concierge remembers your name unprompted on day two, whether the pool chairs actually get cleaned between guests. I've spent mornings at every property on this list, usually with a coffee and a notebook, watching how each place handles the moment when guests arrive with expectations.

At the top of the range, the InterContinental Sun Peninsula does things that no other hotel in Vietnam attempts — a funicular down a jungle cliff to a private bay, a MICHELIN restaurant perched above the South China Sea, a bathroom with a life-size ostrich sculpture next to a hand-carved stone bath. Whether that's absurd or magnificent depends on your mood, but I've never met anyone who regretted staying there. At the other end, the Brilliant Hotel on the Han River does something equally difficult: a riverside room with Dragon Bridge in the window, under $50, and a front desk team that arranges dragon boat tours and remembers to tell you when the fire show is on. The beach strip in between — Four Points Sheraton, TMS Hotel, A La Carte — is where most first-timers land, and if they picked well, they don't regret it.

Luxury Resort

InterContinental Danang Sun Peninsula Resort

I came around a bend on the jungle road and the whole thing just appeared below me — four infinity pools stepping down a cliff to a private white-sand bay, the South China Sea going on forever behind it. You take a funicular to reach the beach, a carved wooden car styled like a traditional fishing boat. The bathroom in my villa had a life-size ostrich sculpture standing beside a hand-carved stone bath, which nobody explains and which I found oddly right. When I got a bad cold mid-stay, the team delivered panadol, cough tablets, a pot of ginger tea, complimentary congee, and a handwritten card. La Maison 1888 was serving Dublin Bay langoustine and Japanese wild blackfin seabass with caviar the night I ate there. The isolation from the city is real — you're a 45-minute drive from anything — but I'd stopped caring about that by day two.

5-Star Resort

Hyatt Regency Danang Resort and Spa

The first thing I noticed at breakfast was the dark chocolate chip cookies on the welcome tray — crumbly with a good chew, the salt cutting through the chocolate in exactly the right way. Then I noticed the banh mi station, which I went back to three mornings running. The beach is 750 metres of Non Nuoc and the five pools spread along it so generously that I rarely had to wait for a sun lounger even during peak season. I'd catch the ocean from the bathtub and the bed at the same time, which sounds like a boast in a brochure but is just true. The kids at Camp Hyatt were divided into Earth, Water, Fire, and Wood zones and running between them with total conviction. About 75 percent of the guests at breakfast were Korean families, which turned out to be a useful sign — they'd found the most complete family beach resort in Da Nang and weren't keeping it quiet.

5-Star Resort

Sheraton Grand Danang Resort

I walked the beachfront at 6:30 in the morning and had it almost entirely to myself. By 9am it was transformed — the main waterslide had a queue of children treating it like a serious personal project, and two separate families had established poolside territories with the kind of territorial precision that only manifests on the third or fourth day of a resort holiday. The breakfast gets praised relentlessly in reviews, and I'd agree — the spread is wide, the pho is properly made, and the croissants were genuinely airy rather than the dense, vaguely croissant-shaped things you find elsewhere. The Kids Club runs a daily programme of actual activities. Parents at the quiet pool on the far side were visibly enjoying the peace of it. If you're travelling with children and want a beachfront resort that actually delivers rather than gestures toward the idea, this is the one.

The beach at the Pullman is quiet — fewer crowds than the northern stretch of My Khe — and the private section has sun loungers with QR codes for ordering drinks without leaving your spot. Boutique Retreat

Naman Retreat Da Nang

The restaurant at Naman is called Hay Hay and it's a bamboo cathedral — soaring columns, hundreds of lanterns hanging from the ceiling, each one shaped like a grain of rice. At the infinity pool bar one evening, a wind came off the sea and moved through the palm trees overhead and I thought, this is the version of a resort holiday that people mean when they say they needed a proper holiday. City Hotel

Novotel Danang Premier Han River

I woke up at 9pm on a Friday, opened the curtains, and the Dragon Bridge was breathing fire directly into my view. Best Mid-Range Hotels in Da Nang

This is the tier where Da Nang's real value story lives. I've watched travellers arrive expecting something serviceable and leave posting photos of rooftop pools and seafood dinners and sunrises over My Khe that they genuinely did not anticipate. A direct beachfront room, a pool, a gym, and breakfast for $65-120 a night. They'd budgeted for a tradeoff and found they'd made one only on paper. These aren't the InterContinental — they don't have the architecture or the MICHELIN dining — but they have the beach, and on most measures that's what people actually came for.

4-Star Beach

Four Points by Sheraton Danang

The honest review from Vietnam Coracle called the lobby "an echoey hangar" and the service "attentive but robotic," and I think that's fair — the Four Points isn't trying to charm you, it's trying not to fail you, and at that it largely succeeds. You cross the street and you're on My Khe Beach. The rooftop pool holds over 100 craft beer varieties at the Horizon bar, which is a specific and genuine selling point if you care about that sort of thing. The Serenity Spa looks out at the sea. The beds are described as "super comfy" in enough reviews to trust it. If you're a Bonvoy member who wants points at a price below the Sheraton Grand's, this is where the maths works in your favour. The seafood restaurants within walking distance are excellent and locally priced in a way that compensates for the hotel's lack of culinary ambition.

City Hotel

Grand Mercure Da Nang

From the room I had at the Grand Mercure, I could see Dragon Bridge, the Han River, the Tran Thi Ly Bridge, and the mountains beyond the city all in a single frame. On a Friday night, the bridge breathed fire and water at 9pm, and watching it from distance was better than watching it from the street below — up close the crowd is dense and noisy, from the hotel it's quiet and the whole spectacle makes more sense. The breakfast ran the widest spread I found anywhere in the city: pho made to order, pastries, hot dishes, the works. The temperature-controlled infinity pool on the ground floor overlooks the Tran Thi Ly Bridge and gets afternoon sun. One reviewer noticed a rotten-egg smell near the pool during their specific visit, which turned out to be coming from near the bridge rather than the hotel itself — worth knowing. The furniture in the rooms is a little crammed together by the windows, but the windows are the reason you're there.

Wyndham Soleil Danang

The Wyndham Soleil is newer than most of what surrounds it on My Khe and carries itself accordingly — the rooms are clean and contemporary without overclaiming, the pool area has a pool bar where you can order a cold beer without getting out of the water, and the beach is a minute's walk. 5-Star Resort

Furama Resort Danang

Furama opened in 1997 and the difference between it and every newer resort on the coast is visible in the gardens. Three decades of tropical growth make the grounds feel like something genuine rather than recently purchased — you walk through them and the overhead canopy is thick, the saltwater lagoon pool is enormous and set deep within it. This resort holds the only Culinary Heritage Asia-Pacific designation in Vietnam, which means the cooking programme here has a seriousness that most resorts' restaurants pretend to. Guests who know Da Nang well choose Furama specifically because it doesn't try to be flashy about any of this — no grand claims, just the weight of the place. The Marble Mountains are 15 minutes by taxi, close enough to visit in the morning and be back before the pool fills up. The beach at Bac My An is calmer than My Khe, which for some guests is the deciding factor.

Vietnamese Brand

Muong Thanh Luxury Da Nang Hotel

Muong Thanh is a Vietnamese hotel brand, which means it understands what Vietnamese guests want from a beach hotel and has calibrated accordingly: clean rooms, a rooftop bar with city views, a functioning outdoor pool, a spa that does good massage at prices that don't require advance negotiation, and a location on My Khe that puts you on the sand in under a minute. Why mid-range hotels are popular in Da Nang

I've had this conversation with travellers who've done Southeast Asia extensively and the reaction is usually the same: a direct beachfront room on My Khe Beach with a pool, a breakfast buffet, and clean linen costs $65-120 a night. In Phuket, the identical experience costs $150-250. In Bali, more. The Four Points Sheraton, TMS Hotel, and Muong Thanh Luxury are popular in part because the savings let people extend the trip by a few nights and spend the evenings at Dragon Bridge rather than doing the mental arithmetic of whether they can afford another dinner.

Common Questions

Da Nang Hotel Reviews: FAQ

Which hotel in Da Nang is the best? +

Honestly, the InterContinental Danang Sun Peninsula Resort — a funicular through jungle, four infinity pools above a private bay, a MICHELIN-starred restaurant, and a level of considered design that takes most resorts three decades to accidentally achieve. If the price is outside your range, the Hyatt Regency Danang is the most reliably excellent luxury resort on the beach for families, and the Sheraton Grand Danang runs the strongest kids programme in the city. At the mid-range, I keep coming back to the A La Carte Da Nang Beach — that rooftop infinity pool at sunset makes an argument that's hard to counter at $75 a night.

What is the average hotel price in Da Nang? +

Budget guesthouses in the city centre start around $20-40 a night. The My Khe Beach mid-range tier — clean rooms, pool, beachfront — runs $55-120, which is where I'd steer most first-time visitors. You get more hotel for the money here than anywhere else in Southeast Asia at this price band. Beachfront resorts with full resort facilities — Pullman, Hilton, Furama — sit at $130-220 depending on season. The luxury tier starts around $250 and goes past $600 for the InterContinental and Naman Retreat's best villas. Rates in Da Nang shift significantly between seasons — November through January can run 30-40 percent cheaper than peak summer. See our full hotel price guide for month-by-month breakdowns.

Do Da Nang hotels include breakfast? +

It depends on the property and rate type. Most 4-5 star hotels on My Khe offer breakfast-included rates alongside room-only options. The buffets at the Hyatt Regency, Sheraton Grand, and Hilton are substantial enough that the add-on is usually worth it — the Hyatt's pho and banh mi stations alone justify it. Fusion Suites includes all-day breakfast as standard in every rate, which is unusual and genuinely changes the value calculation. At the Brilliant and Novotel, the breakfast spread gets strong reviews and is often included in the published rate. At guesthouses and budget hotels, breakfast typically isn't included, but this matters less than it might elsewhere — local Vietnamese breakfast from the banh mi cart outside your hotel costs $1 and is usually better than what a budget hotel kitchen would produce anyway.

Is it safe to book hotels in Da Nang online? +

Yes, without reservation. I've booked online in Da Nang dozens of times and the process is clean and reliable. Booking.com has the widest inventory here and the cancellation policies are displayed accurately — most properties offer free cancellation on standard rates, which makes locking something in early a sensible move. Agoda occasionally runs cheaper rates on the same property, worth cross-checking. All the hotels on this page link directly to their Booking.com listings so you can check live pricing against your dates. For the luxury resorts — InterContinental, Naman, TIA — it's worth calling the hotel directly after you've found your rate online; they'll sometimes match the price and add an upgrade or early check-in. For everything else, book online and don't overthink it.
