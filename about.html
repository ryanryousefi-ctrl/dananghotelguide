About Da Nang Hotel Guide

This is the guide I wish existed the first time I landed here.

Ryan Yousefi
Founder & Editor · Da Nang, Vietnam

Meet the Founder

Ryan Yousefi

I first came to Vietnam in 2009, and Da Nang kept pulling me back more times than I could count. Eventually I stopped fighting it and moved here.

My wife is Vietnamese, we have a son, and this is home now.

I've worked as a journalist for over 20 years, covering everything from food and hospitality to sports and breaking news. By trade I've also worked in communications and management, with a career that's run through both hotel operations and healthcare in Florida.

That combination gives me a different read on this than most travel writers get. I know how hotels actually run behind the desk, so I know the difference between a property that photographs well and one that actually delivers once you're checked in.

A journalist's skepticism, some hospitality industry knowledge, and years of living in Da Nang through every season: that's what this guide is built on.

The Purpose

Why This Guide Exists

For years I watched the same questions cycle through Da Nang Facebook groups week after week. Where should I stay? Is the beach near this hotel actually good? What's there to do besides the obvious tourist stuff? Visitors, expats, even locals, all chasing the same information, scattered across comment threads that disappeared into the algorithm by morning.

How We Review Hotels

Every hotel on this site gets evaluated against the same criteria: location accuracy (is it as close to the beach as it claims?), value for money at the current booking price, an honest read on who the hotel suits and who it doesn't, and pool or beach quality, which matters enormously in Da Nang.

I don't accept free stays in exchange for coverage. Hotels are assessed using publicly available booking prices, on-site visits where I've been there personally, and verified guest feedback patterns.

When a hotel has a consistent weakness, road noise, slow Wi-Fi, aggressive upselling, I say so directly.

Why I Built This

I moved to Da Nang in 2022 after two decades in journalism. When I arrived, the English-language hotel coverage fell into two camps: SEO-optimized filler from sites that had clearly never set foot here, or outdated posts from bloggers who'd passed through for a weekend back in 2018.

Neither was useful.

Da Nang has changed a lot since then: new resorts, a completely rebuilt coastal road, the annual DIFF fireworks festival that now fills every riverside hotel for weeks, a Korean tourism boom that reshuffled which neighborhoods matter. None of it was being documented accurately in English.

So I started writing it myself. The goal was simple: give travelers the same honest advice I give people who email me asking where to stay.

Not affiliate-optimized rankings. Not recycled press releases.

Just here's what I actually know, here's what I'd actually book, here's what I'd actually avoid.

At some point it became clear this information needed a permanent home. Not another generic listicle stitched together from a press trip, but something built by someone who actually lives here: someone who knows which stretches of My Khe go choppy in the afternoon wind, which "beachfront" hotels are genuinely on the sand and which ones use that word loosely from across a service road, and which properties have quietly slipped since their last round of glowing reviews.

This guide isn't only for people still deciding where to book. It's for guests who've already chosen a hotel but land feeling overwhelmed, unsure what to do, where to eat, how to get around.

Da Nang is a lively, beautiful place with very few flaws. The biggest one, honestly, has been the lack of a proper visitor's guide.

That's what this is.

If you're already here, welcome. If you're still on the fence about coming, what are you waiting for?

How We Work

Editorial Standards

Twenty years in journalism leaves you with a healthy distrust of official narratives, and hotel marketing copy is no different. The habits carry over: verify the claim, assess what's actually being delivered versus what's being promised, and don't let a polished press release stand in for a real look.

When a property has slipped, I say so. When a small guesthouse punches above its weight against a big brand name, I say that too.

Some links on this site are affiliate links, mainly Booking.com. When you book through one, I earn a small commission at no extra cost to you.

I'll always be upfront about that, but the affiliate relationship doesn't decide what gets recommended, how it's ranked, or what I write about it.

These are the hotels I'd steer a well-traveled friend toward, commission or not.
