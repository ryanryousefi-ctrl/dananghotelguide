Naman Retreat Da Nang

“The beach, the pool and the bamboo architecture are genuinely special. The high-rise room I stayed in was not.”

5-Star Boutique Retreat · Non Nuoc Beach

Our Take

I wanted to love Naman. I'd visited before, under different circumstances, spending time with friends who were staying in one of the resort's villas, and I left with a strong impression of the place: the bamboo architecture, the long pool, the sense that someone had actually thought about this property rather than ordering it from a catalog. That visit set expectations that my own stay, in one of Naman's high-rise rooms rather than a villa, didn't entirely meet.

That distinction matters more than almost anything else in this review. Naman Retreat sells two fairly different experiences under one name. The villas are the ones in the photographs that made the resort's reputation. The high-rise accommodation, where I stayed this time, is a different product, and the gap between the two is wide enough that it changes who this hotel is actually right for.

Nothing here was broken or unclean. The complaints that follow are about feel and consistency, not hygiene or function. But feel is most of what you're paying a premium for at a design-led resort, and on that measure, the room let the rest of the property down.

Arrival

Check-In Felt Like a Wellness Retreat, Not a Five-Star Lobby

My first impression on walking in was that this didn't feel like stepping into the marble lobby of a conventional five-star resort, or even a well-kept three-star business hotel. It felt closer to arriving at a wellness retreat: quiet, understated, more wood paneling than polish. The reception area was functional rather than luxurious, and parts of it read as a little dated rather than intentionally minimal.

Credit where it's due: check-in itself was fast and to the point. Staff were helpful, there was no standing around waiting for someone to find a reservation, and within a few minutes a golf cart was already arranged to take us to our room.

That golf cart turned out to matter. Our room was in the high-rise section of the property, far enough from the main reception and resort core that walking there with luggage wouldn't have been realistic. It was an early, physical hint at how different the experience can be depending on which category of accommodation you've actually booked.

The Grounds

Beautiful Grounds, and a Building That Clearly Isn't the Point

Whatever else is true about the room, the walk to get there was genuinely pleasant. Shaded paths lined with dense greenery wind between the villas, staff cycle between buildings rather than drive, and the whole property has an unhurried, settled quality that doesn't come across in photos of just the pool or the restaurant. This part of Naman's reputation is earned.

But walking those paths also made something obvious: Naman leans on its villa real estate, and the high-rise building reads like an afterthought bolted onto a villa resort rather than a planned part of it. The villas get the prime, closer-in positioning and the design attention. The high-rise sits apart, a separate structure with its own logic, worse maintained, less visited.

How separate became clear over the course of the stay. We saw exactly one other guest in the entire high-rise building the whole time we were there. Whether that's low occupancy, a layout that keeps high-rise guests from crossing paths with each other, or simply how few rooms in that category get booked relative to the villas, I can't say for certain. But a nearly empty building is its own kind of answer about where Naman's actual attention goes.

The Room, Candidly

If You Can Afford the Difference, You May Regret Not Booking a Villa

That was my honest reaction walking into the high-rise accommodation. Not because the room was dirty or unusable, it wasn't, but because it felt dramatically less special than the villa side of this resort. As someone from South Florida, my first impression reminded me more of a Pompano Beach condo from the 1990s than a room inside a design-led Vietnamese beach resort.

The contrast with the rest of the property was jarring. Naman's bamboo architecture, its villas, its public spaces, all of that is genuinely distinctive. This particular accommodation category felt second-class next to it, and I want to use that phrase carefully rather than for effect: the room simply didn't carry the same level of design intention as everything surrounding it.

The furniture felt somewhat randomly assembled rather than deliberately chosen. The kitchen and living area were sparse, counters were close to bare, and there wasn't even a coffee maker sitting out. What struck me most was how uneven it felt room to room: the bedroom was substantially more modern and hotel-like, the bathroom could have belonged to a different, newer resort entirely, while the living room and kitchenette felt older and far less polished. The different parts of the room almost read as though they'd been designed in completely different eras.

The dining table, if you can call it that, was the clearest example. It was closer to a card table than a kitchen table, small enough that it barely held a single order of Grab delivery for two people. Vietnam is well known for producing high-quality furniture, cheaply, which made the choice feel like a decision rather than a budget constraint. Certain parts of this room made a clear point about how little thought went into them.

The Bedroom and Bathroom Told a Different Story Than the Living Room

It's worth being specific here, because the room wasn't uniformly dated, it was unevenly updated. The bedroom felt current: clean lines, a proper bed, a sea-facing window with sheer curtains that actually let in good light. The bed itself was large enough that two adults and a child could have slept in it comfortably, which should give you a sense of the actual size. Not every bed labeled king is built to the same dimensions, and this one clearly was.

The bathroom almost felt like it had been updated recently while the rest of the room stayed stuck in 2002. The tub and toilet looked brand new, genuinely nice fixtures. It was a strange kind of jarring, the sort of detail that makes you stop and notice things just don't line up with each other.

Step back into the living and kitchen area, though, and the years show. None of this makes the room unusable. The better way to frame it is simply: know what you're booking. A high-rise room at Naman is a comfortable, serviceable place to sleep. It is not the architectural experience the resort is known for.

Who the High-Rise Room Suits

It Makes More Sense If You're Barely in the Room

This room category makes much more sense for travelers who plan to spend nearly all their waking hours at the pool, on the beach, in the restaurants, or out exploring Da Nang and Hoi An. If you treat the room primarily as a comfortable place to shower and sleep, its shortcomings matter far less. If you want the room itself to feel like part of the vacation, the kind of space you're happy to linger in during an afternoon rain shower, I'd strongly consider paying the difference for a villa instead.

Pool & Pool Bar

A Beautiful Pool, Fairly Hands-Off Service

Visually, the pool is one of the strongest parts of the resort. It runs long and straight, palms lining both sides, the ocean waiting at the far end, and it photographs exactly as well in person as it does online. As a piece of design, nothing about it disappointed.

The service experience around it didn't quite match the setting. The area felt somewhat understaffed, and it became apparent fairly quickly that guests were largely expected to look after themselves. There were no menus waiting at our poolside table. Rather than sit and wait, I eventually walked over to the pool bar myself and ordered the buy-one-get-one drinks on offer.

The drinks themselves were perfectly fine for the price, doing exactly what a beach-resort cocktail is supposed to do: make sitting beside the water a little more enjoyable. But this wasn't the kind of high-end resort pool experience where staff circulate constantly, anticipate what you need, and keep drinks and menus coming without being asked. It's a beautiful pool area with fairly hands-off service, worth knowing going in rather than discovering on day one.

The Beach

Simple, Peaceful, and Close Enough to the Pool to Move Between Both

The beach was one of the highlights of the stay, mainly because there wasn't much to complain about. It was simple, peaceful and quiet: hammocks strung between posts, open seating, a small beach-games setup with darts and an archery target, and a long stretch of open sand with the ocean doing most of the work.

It carries the familiar appeal of Da Nang's quieter beach resorts, where the scenery does the heavy lifting rather than organized activity. The beach is also close enough to the pool that you can realistically shuttle between the two throughout the day without it feeling like a production, which was one of the nicer, lower-key parts of the property.

Dinner

We Skipped the BBQ Buffet, and Here's Why

We didn't end up eating the resort's "Oceantastic BBQ" buffet dinner, and I want to be transparent about why rather than let it read as a quiet criticism. Because of rain that evening, the outdoor BBQ setup had been moved indoors. It runs Wednesdays and Saturdays, 6 to 9pm, and the signboard lists two options: roughly $51 per person for one grilled Wagyu beef course plus a single drink, or roughly $63 per person for the same Wagyu course with a free-flow beverage package and a live band.

If you're in Da Nang for one night chasing a luxury splurge, that price probably doesn't register as a factor either way. But if you've eaten at comparable hotel BBQ nights elsewhere and know what that budget usually buys, the math here was a pass for us. We decided we'd probably enjoy ourselves just as much ordering Grab delivery to the room and winding down after a full day in the sun. That's a value call about our mood, the format, and the price that night, not a verdict on the food itself, which we didn't eat and won't pretend to review.

Breakfast

The Clear Highlight of the Food and Beverage Side

Breakfast is where Naman's food and beverage operation made a far better impression than the room had. The spread was broad and generous, with plenty of options well beyond the standard hotel buffet, and a couple of memorable touches: a chocolate fondue tower, unusual to see at breakfast, and a fun, slightly theatrical orange slush-style machine that guests kept returning to.

The dining space itself deserves attention on its own, arched bamboo columns rising into a vaulted roofline hung with lanterns, visually in a different league from the high-rise room I'd walked out of that morning. It's the strongest evidence in the whole stay that Naman's design reputation is earned, just not evenly distributed across every part of the resort.

One caveat worth flagging: we overheard other guests at breakfast discussing what they'd paid for it, which suggests it's sold as a standalone, walk-in purchase to people not actually staying at the resort. A little digging afterward turned up the same thing, that this is at least sometimes the case. That would explain something that otherwise didn't add up: the resort itself felt lightly occupied the rest of the time, yet breakfast suddenly felt like it had 200 people in the room, with lines at the hot stations long enough that we gave up and skipped them some mornings. In the end the breakfast itself was there and worth the money. The crowding just isn't something the resort should be bragging about.

A Small Thing Worth Mentioning

The Coffee Situation Was Oddly Inconsistent

It's a small detail, but it says something about execution. The coffee setup at breakfast was somewhat chaotic: some guests were apparently told staff no longer took coffee orders at the table, while I and other guests simply walked up to the café counter and ordered directly. A few of the milk-based drinks appeared to have been made in advance and left sitting, waiting for someone to claim them, which meant a lukewarm latte rather than the freshly made one you'd expect at a resort breakfast.

The Americano situation was stranger still. Instead of a freshly pulled espresso-based Americano, one of the things I normally look forward to at a hotel breakfast, what appeared to be on offer was effectively coffee from a pot. I passed and chose something else. None of this is worth getting worked up over, but it's a fair example of where Naman's execution doesn't quite match its positioning.

Design & Atmosphere

The Public Spaces Still Look Better Than the High-Rise Room Does

None of the above should overshadow what Naman still does well. The bamboo architecture remains visually distinctive, the restaurant in particular is genuinely beautiful, and the resort becomes especially attractive at night, when lanterns and warm lighting take over the walkways and the grounds. The landscaping, the palms, the paths threading through the property, the ocean-facing areas, all of it explains exactly why Naman photographs so well and why its reputation drew me back for a second visit.

That contrast is the whole story of this review: the public areas can look significantly more impressive than the high-rise room product sitting a short golf-cart ride away from them.

What We Like

What We Like

The beach and the pool

A long, photogenic infinity pool and a quiet, simple beach, close enough together that moving between them all day is effortless.

Bamboo architecture

The restaurant and public spaces are built from vaulted bamboo structures unlike anything else on this stretch of coast.

Breakfast

A genuinely generous spread, served inside the resort's most striking room, with a few memorable touches beyond the standard buffet.

Quiet, pleasant grounds

Shaded garden paths and a calm, unhurried pace that suits a slow, settle-in kind of trip.

Location

Location

Non Nuoc Beach, about 13km and a 25-minute taxi from Da Nang's city centre, with Hoi An roughly 25 minutes south, easier for day trips than most city-centre hotels. In practice, that means you're not stepping out for street food or wandering into town on a whim, this is a destination stay built around settling into the property rather than using it as a base for city exploring. The Marble Mountains are a five-minute taxi ride, and TIA Wellness and Furama sit nearby on the same quiet stretch if you want a change of scenery for a meal.

Would We Stay Here Again

ONLY IN A VILLA

I wanted to love Naman, and in a lot of ways I still do, just not unconditionally. My earlier visit, around friends staying in one of the villas, left me with a genuinely glowing impression of this resort, and that experience probably raised my expectations more than was fair. Actually staying here myself, specifically in the high-rise accommodation, was a far more forgettable experience than that first impression promised.

The resort still has real strengths: an attractive beach, a beautiful main pool, distinctive bamboo architecture, a strong breakfast, a quiet setting, and pleasant grounds throughout. The villa product, based on what I saw on that earlier visit, appears substantially more compelling than the room I actually stayed in this time. If you're booking Naman because you've fallen for photographs of its architecture and villas, make sure you're actually booking the experience shown in those photographs, not every room category delivers the same feeling. Booking the high-rise accommodation as a base for enjoying the beach and pool can still make sense. Booking it expecting a polished five-star room and attentive five-star service is where I'd temper expectations.
