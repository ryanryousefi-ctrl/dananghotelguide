Why Expats Choose Da Nang

Da Nang sits in a compelling middle ground that neither Ho Chi Minh City nor Hanoi quite offers: the cost of living is meaningfully lower than both, the beach is 10 minutes from the city centre, the air quality is better, and it's genuinely manageable in size. A city of roughly 1.3 million people — large enough to have proper infrastructure and an international food scene, small enough that you don't spend an hour in traffic every day.

The expat community is concentrated around a few main pockets: the An Thuong neighbourhood (the most Western-friendly, often called "the expat area"), the My Khe beachfront strip, and the newer developments near the Dragon Bridge and Han River waterfront. Most expats here are either remote workers, English teachers, retirees, or business owners in tourism-adjacent sectors.

Da Nang's weaknesses for expats are real too: limited specialist medical care (most serious conditions require a flight to Ho Chi Minh City or Bangkok), a smaller social scene than larger cities, limited international schooling options, and a language barrier that's more pronounced than in the expat-heavy Thao Dien neighbourhood of HCMC. None of these are dealbreakers — but they're worth factoring in.

Rent in Da Nang: What Your Money Gets (2026)

Da Nang's rental market has risen noticeably since 2022, partly due to increased expat demand and partly because of broader Vietnamese urban property inflation. That said, it remains significantly cheaper than HCMC's expat neighbourhoods or any comparable Southeast Asian beach city. Here's what the market looks like in 2026:

💡 Rental Market Reality

Most expat rentals are negotiated directly with Vietnamese landlords — Facebook groups like "Da Nang Expats" and "Da Nang Housing" are the primary discovery channel, not real estate agents. Prices in these groups are usually lower than what agents quote. Leases are typically 6 or 12 months. Many landlords prefer cash upfront for 3–6 months for better rates.

Expect to pay 1–2 months deposit. Utility bills (electricity, water) are added separately and can be significant in summer when air conditioning runs constantly — budget an extra $60–100/month for a 1-bedroom in peak heat.

Food is where Da Nang shines for expats on a budget. Eating almost exclusively at local Vietnamese restaurants costs less than $8–12 per day including coffee, beer, and three meals. Transport in Da Nang

Da Nang is a motorbike city. The most practical and affordable way for expats to get around is to buy or rent a semi-automatic or automatic motorbike. The city is flat, well-laid-out, and parking is easy compared to HCMC or Hanoi.

Motorbike options

Buying: A decent second-hand Honda Wave (manual, very common) costs $300–500. A used Honda Air Blade or Vision (automatic, easier for newcomers) runs $700–1,200. Budget for registration and a local motorbike licence or international driving permit. Monthly running costs (fuel, maintenance, parking) run $40–70.

Renting long-term: Monthly motorbike hire from reputable shops runs $60–90/month for an automatic scooter. Good for a trial period before buying.

Grab and taxis

Grab (Southeast Asia's Uber equivalent) is cheap and reliable in Da Nang. A Grab motorbike across the city costs $0.80–$2.50; a Grab car is $2–6. Budget expats use Grab for convenience or bad weather; most own bikes for daily commuting.

Car rental

Monthly car rental runs $500–900/month for a basic sedan. Few expats maintain full-time car rentals given motorbike culture and parking costs, but it's common for families with children or anyone doing regular longer trips up to Hoi An and Hue.

Healthcare in Da Nang

Healthcare in Da Nang has improved significantly but remains the city's most significant expat limitation. Here's the honest picture:

For routine care: Family Medical Practice Da Nang and several international-standard clinics handle GP visits, dental work, minor illnesses, and lab tests to a good standard. Expect $30–80 for a consultation at an international clinic.

For serious conditions: Da Nang Hospital (Da Nang General Hospital) handles emergencies, but for anything requiring specialist surgery or complex diagnosis, most expats fly to Ho Chi Minh City (Vinmec International, FV Hospital) or Bangkok. This is a genuine consideration — factor medical evacuation insurance into your budget.

Dental: Da Nang has excellent and affordable dental care by international standards. Routine cleaning: $15–30. Crowns: $150–350. Implants: $600–1,200.

Health insurance: Budget $80–200/month for a comprehensive expat health insurance plan covering medical evacuation and hospital care. This is non-negotiable if you're staying long-term.

International Schools in Da Nang

Da Nang's international school options are limited compared to HCMC or Hanoi — this is one of the city's genuine weaknesses for families with school-age children. As of 2026, the main options are:

  • International School of Da Nang (ISD): The largest and most established option. IB curriculum. Annual fees roughly $8,000–14,000 USD depending on year level.
  • Horizon International Bilingual School: Vietnamese-English bilingual curriculum. More affordable, roughly $4,000–7,000/year. Better suited to families intending longer stays who want local integration.
  • British International School Da Nang: British curriculum. Fees in the $10,000–16,000/year range for older years.

If you have children at secondary school level and require strong university pathway preparation, HCMC or Hanoi are realistically better choices for international education options.

Internet and Connectivity

Da Nang has fast, reliable internet — better than many Western cities and significantly better than most of Southeast Asia at comparable price points. A home fibre connection through Viettel, VNPT, or FPT runs $12–25/month for speeds of 100–500 Mbps. Mobile data through Vietnamobile, Viettel, or Vinaphone is cheap and fast: 4G SIM cards with 20–30 GB of data cost around $5–10/month.

Co-working spaces are available throughout the city for those working remotely — see the Da Nang digital nomad guide for full details on workspaces, cafés, and connectivity by neighbourhood.

Visas for Long-Term Expats

Vietnam's visa system for long-term foreign residents remains complex and evolving. The main options as of 2026:

  • E-visa (EV): 90-day multiple-entry visa, extendable once for another 90 days. Available for most nationalities. Best for initial moves and trial stays.
  • Visa exemption: Citizens of many countries (UK, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, and others) receive 45-day visa-free entry. Check current exemption lists as these change.
  • 1-year business visa (DN visa): Requires a Vietnamese business sponsor. The standard long-term option for many expats.
  • Temporary residence card (TRC): For those married to Vietnamese citizens or employed by Vietnamese companies.
  • Border runs: Da Nang's proximity to Laos makes Lao Bao border a common option. See the Da Nang visa run guide for details.
⚠️ Visa Disclaimer

Vietnam's visa regulations change with limited notice. The information above reflects general conditions as of early 2026 but should not be used as legal advice. Consult a reputable local immigration agent for your specific nationality and situation. Lynn Visa Da Nang is well-regarded among the local expat community for English-language immigration support.

Banking and Money in Da Nang

Opening a Vietnamese bank account as a foreigner requires a valid visa and passport. Techcombank and BIDV are the most expat-friendly options, with English-language apps and international transfers. The process typically takes 1–3 days. Most expats maintain a home country account for international transfers and a local VND account for daily spending.

ATMs are widely available across the city. VCB (Vietcombank) and BIDV ATMs have the lowest foreign card fees. Budget for $3–6 in withdrawal fees per transaction if drawing on international cards — many expats do weekly rather than daily withdrawals to minimise these.

Is Da Nang Cheaper Than Chiang Mai, Bali, or Ho Chi Minh City?

The honest comparison: Da Nang is comparable to Chiang Mai in overall cost, slightly cheaper than Bali at mid-range lifestyle levels (Bali's premium areas like Canggu have risen significantly), and about 15–25% cheaper than Ho Chi Minh City for equivalent-quality accommodation and dining. The beach access is superior to all of these for swimming quality, and the city is significantly more manageable in size than HCMC.

What Da Nang lacks vs. HCMC: diversity of international cuisine, volume of specialist services, nightlife depth, and international airport connections. For a remote worker or retiree who values beach lifestyle, lower stress, and a genuine Vietnamese city experience — Da Nang consistently comes out ahead.
