Unlike the people in Da Nang -- the weather is moody and at times, downright inhospitable. Got plans to go to Ba Na Hills? Too bad. Typhoon warning. Want to see Lady Buddha during a 48-hour stopover? Sorry -- it's pouring outside. Looking to spend a full day on the beach? Nice try -- you'll last two hours at most, because it's hotter than the surface of the sun.

Weather in Southeast Asia as a whole is a book in and of itself, but knowing what to expect in Da Nang can make or break a trip. Come prepared. Come when it makes the most sense. And in the end -- may the odds be ever in your favor.

The good news? Da Nang is just as beautiful inside as it is out.

The Short Version

Da Nang has four distinct seasons. Here's the one-paragraph version of each.

Season 1 Peak beach
Dry Season
Feb – May
Warm days, calm seas, minimal rain. March and April are the sweet spot — 28–30°C, 5–6 rain days per month, and beach water that's genuinely swimmable. This is when to come.
Season 2 Peak heat
Summer
Jun – Aug
Hot and dry — 33°C with full sun. Beach weather, but plan around midday heat. Domestic tourism peaks in July–August. Sea hits 30°C. Book early; hotels fill up fast on weekends.
Season 3 Monsoon
Wet Season
Sep – Nov
October averages 620mm — more than London's annual rainfall in one month. September and November are manageable for city visits. October: reschedule if you can. Hoi An floods badly.
Season 4 Cool & quiet
Cool Season
Dec – Jan
23°C highs, occasional showers, fewer tourists. Not a beach trip, but the food scene and city exploration work well. Good value before the dry season crowds arrive in February.

Editor's recommendation
Editor's Pick

March & April: The Two Best Months

22mmMarch rain
28–30°Cdaily highs
5–6rain days
Calmseas

March is the driest month of the year — 22mm total, 5 rain days, 28°C, calm sea, empty weekday beach. If I had to send one friend at one time, March is it. April adds a couple more rain days and edges to 30°C but loses nothing essential. The two-week window from late March through mid-April is as good as Da Nang gets.

Fewer tourists than summer, hotel prices not yet at peak, and the Hoi An day trip weather is also ideal during this window.

February: The Underrated Month

Most people skip February because it follows January's rain. That's wrong. By February, the monsoon is fully gone — 28mm of rain, 7 rain days, 25°C. The sea is a bit cooler than peak summer but perfectly swimmable. Weekday beaches are empty. Hotels cost 20–30% less than March peak. If you can fly in early February and avoid Tet (Vietnamese New Year, usually late January or early February), you'll have a near-empty, near-perfect Da Nang.

The one caveat: check the Tet dates for your year — restaurants close for several days and the city goes quiet in a way that's either atmospheric or inconvenient depending on your disposition.

Sea Temperature

The South China Sea at Da Nang

Da Nang's sea temperature is warm enough to swim comfortably for most of the year. The peak window is May through September when surface temps hit 28–30°C. Even in the "cool" months (January–February) the water rarely drops below 23°C — cold by Da Nang standards, fine for swimming by most other standards.

Context

What Rain Actually Means Here

The difference between 66mm and 620mm

Rainfall averages hide the real picture. 66mm in January (spread across 14 days) means occasional showers — grey sky, some drizzle, then it clears. 620mm in October (21 rain days) means sustained heavy downpours, flooding in low-lying areas, and beach conditions that are simply unsafe.

What matters more than the millimeter number: how many consecutive dry hours you typically get. In the dry season (March–May), you'll regularly get 48+ hours without a drop. In October, you won't go more than a day without significant rain.

June and July have 76–82mm — comparable to many European summer destinations — but because it falls as afternoon thunderstorms rather than all-day drizzle, the beach mornings are typically excellent.

Typhoons: what you actually need to know

Da Nang sits in the typhoon belt. The risk window is August–November, with October and November being historically highest risk. Most years, nothing major hits directly. But "most years" is not a plan. If you're visiting September–November, get travel insurance that covers weather disruption. It's cheap and removes a real risk.

The 1997 and 2017 storms were serious. Infrastructure has improved since. The risk hasn't gone away.

FAQ

Da Nang Weather FAQs

What is the driest month in Da Nang?
March, with about 22mm of rain for the whole month. That's genuinely dry — not "it might not rain" dry, but actual stretches of days where it simply doesn't rain at all. You also get 28°C temperatures, calm water, and beach conditions that are as good as it gets. If I had to pick one month to send someone, March is usually it.
Does Da Nang get typhoons?
Yes, occasionally. The window to watch is August through November, with October and November being the highest-risk months historically. Most years nothing major hits directly, but "most years" isn't the same as "it never happens." If you're visiting in that window, get travel insurance that covers weather disruption. It's not expensive and it removes a genuine risk from the equation.
Is Da Nang too hot in summer?
Depends how you handle heat. July and August are 33°C with humidity — that's legitimately intense at midday. But shade works here: step under a tree, an umbrella, or an awning and you feel it immediately. Adjust your timing (beach before 10am, back out after 4pm) and you manage the summer heat comfortably. The 30°C sea temperature is also great for extended swimming. Just don't plan to be in direct sun from noon to 3pm and expect to enjoy it.
How does Da Nang weather affect Hoi An day trips?
October and early November are when Hoi An flooding gets serious — the Hoai River overflows and parts of the Ancient Town can be underwater for days. September also has flood risk. For Hoi An day trips, March through June is the best window: dry, comfortable, the old town is gorgeous in that light. December through February works too once the monsoon clears, though it can be overcast. Never book non-refundable Hoi An accommodation for October.
